## 2. The engine: what Spilsbury wants

He wants **the bond — a positive case under observation.** He has had half of it since he read the Andersen aloud in 1954 and the other half since the contact series. What he has never had is the thing itself, happening, in front of him.

The arc is the want finding a target, being thwarted, and finding a worse one.

**Part One (1960).** Gamma's death hands him the loud end on a plate: a widowed adult male and a sensitive man, and nobody did it on purpose, so none of his three reasons against the separation study applies. He buys it with the best conservation outcome the field has ever had — an eighteen-month suspension of the Trinidad trade — and the price is standing at the rail. He never asks Nestor to do anything. He doesn't need to. *He is dangerous here by being generous at exactly the right moment,* and everybody at the Martin takes the gift knowing what it's for.

**Part Two (1962–63).** Nestor refuses the vow and a female arrives through the transfer exemption Spilsbury himself conceded, certain there was nobody to transfer. The want does not go away; it moves. He takes out the Port of Spain consignment records he bought for forty pounds in 1957 and reads them with the want in his hand, and finds a channel off the Serpent's Mouth where the take collapsed in the season after May 1954 — nets cut, animals lost, and a note in a boatman's hand about *el ahogado*, the drowned man. The Commission's first source-declared returns (Reggie's own condition three, 1955) confirm it. **Reggie's protective instrument is the search pattern.**

His offer, when it comes, is monstrous because it is good: bring Charles and Monty in, legally, under Hester's own provision — a post for life for any bonded member of staff, the animal's company at his own — to the reference station, under Tar's conditions, and incidentally satisfying clause eleven for thirty years. A man of thirty-three who has lived nine years in a delta will be dead of it by forty. It is the one offer that tempts every person who loves Charles, for a different reason each. *That is how he'd take him apart:* not with a net, but by making the pen the kind thing to do.

**What stops him** has to be practical, never moral:

1. Reggie refuses the licence. Spilsbury expected yes, because in November 1958 Tar said yes to him on a verandah without reaching for Pobil, and Spilsbury read that as Tar coming into his own. He does not know Tar went home and told Pobil on the day, as promised, and that the refusal was made at a kitchen table by two people. **This is the gap between what he can deduce and how they actually work, mattering in the end.**
2. Refused, he takes a holiday in Trinidad, which is not going round Tar on the record.
3. Charles himself stops him, on a beach at Cedros, with the one argument Spilsbury respects: if anybody comes for them, Charles walks into Port of Spain and gives a newspaper his name. Every paper in the world asks whether merfolk are people, and the field closes for forty years — the whaling outcome, the thing Spilsbury has told Tar in writing he fears most. Spilsbury believes him because Spilsbury always knows what lands, and Charles means it. Forty years beats one subject. He goes home. Unredeemed, undefeated, and he got the answer to his question — *it is both of us* — from the only man who could give it, and can never print it.

Spilsbury cannot account for a sensitive man declining the only positive case in the world and files it with the other unsolved cards. Then the consignment records.
- **06.** The Colonel dies (VETO? — autumn 1961). What Hester has always known he knew. Reggie accepts Rome. John becomes Director of the Martin. The first calf is expected.

## 4. How each live thread from the handoff closes

1. **Gamma** — dies of age, 11 Feb 1960 (02).
2. **Nestor** — refuses the vow out of care, which mirrors Pascual on the jetty in 1953 (declining Reggie so as not to hang it on him). Stays on the establishment for life; grandchildren by 1984.
3. **Clause eleven** — satisfied first by suspension, then by transfer, then permanently by captive breeding. *Save where no specimen is obtainable* is made untrue not by the trade but by the pens.
4. **Jean** — the exit from Hester's sum; the Florida female; eleven years ending in the understanding of what an estuary has to be; the reintroduction design.
5. **Lisbon** — Teixeira takes the refusal. The separation the four sentences predicted is run in 1962 somewhere with money and no conditions, is uninterpretable per Pobil's paragraphs, is unpublishable, and stops. One paragraph, Part Two.
6. **Rome** — Reggie directs the Commission 1962–c.1975; Pascual becomes its chief veterinary inspector (a man with a badge who can walk into any holding in eleven countries and shut a pen), speaks Spanish at Italians, and is bad-tempered for about a year.
7. **The Colonel** — dies 1961.
8. **Hester** — meets Charles at Cedros; dies c.1968.
9. **Page 206 and the notebook** — published 1984.
10. **Charles** — alive, in the sea, by his own choice, throughout; on the page in 09 and 12.
11. **Reggie and Pascual** — the next thirty years taken at the review, obviously.
12. **Spilsbury's cards** — *AGUILAR — dep. var.?*, *R.T. — block, 4 nights —*, and the last one he makes, which is about why a man would want a brake.

## 5. VETO? — the big inventions

1. **The Trinidad suspension as Spilsbury's price-bearing gift,** and the interim committee of four he chairs.
2. **Nestor refuses the vow.** The alternative — he answers, Hester's provision fires, Chumley never has a mate — is the other real book, and a darker one.
3. **Florida's widowed female, brought by Jean,** and Chumley's line as the root of the captive population.
4. **Monty's people in the Orinoco delta / Gulf of Paria,** across the Serpent's Mouth from the broker's boats. Charles cutting nets.
5. **Spilsbury's offer to bring Charles and Monty in under Hester's provision.**
6. **Reggie never goes to find Charles** — they do not meet until 1984.
7. **Hester travels to Trinidad at sixty-seven** and meets Charles at Cedros.
8. **Charles's threat on the beach** as the practical stop.
9. **The 1984 ending as a release** — captive-born animals back into the water, Charles there to receive them. The original's final image inverted: pens to sea, and a man answering.
10. **The Caño Mánamo closure as the habitat-loss event.** It's real, and it was a catastrophe for Warao communities in the delta, not only for fish. If used, the human cost has to be on the page and not background to a mermaid plot; Charles should not be written as a white man adopted by a tribe. I'd keep him on the margin — trading at Cedros, not living in anybody's village. (Wikipedia gives *manamo* as the Warao word for "two." Worth checking before I lean on it, because if true it's almost too good.)
