# Entire

Oct 2, 2026 · @C

*A companion to "Signal and Noise" and "Write Everything Down": Spilsbury, the summer of 1955.*

## July 1955

He has the fire lit in July.

Mrs Dawes -- she has kept house for him for eleven years, and he has to think, for a moment, for the name -- says nothing about it, which is one of her virtues. She brings the tin boxes down from the attic one at a time, as he asks, and sets them in a row on the hearthrug. There are forty-one. Forty years of notebooks, near enough: small and black and oilcloth-covered, bought from the same stationer's on the Strand since before the first war, each one filled to the last ruled line and dated on the spine in white ink. Murders, mostly, in the early boxes; a great many people who had stopped using their insides, and a great many more who had lied about how. Then the war. Then Deptford.

He sits in the wing chair with a rug over his knees, though the room is warm and the window is open on the square, and looks at them. His hands on the rug are the colour of old ivory. He has mostly stopped looking at them; he knows what they mean. The man in Harley Street said six months, in February. It is the middle of July. He has already outlived his own estimate, if not the kind man's.

The index is in the last box, in a fat volume of its own: every name that has ever gone into a notebook, alphabetical, with box and page. He opens it at T.

*Tar, R. (n=212.)*

Two hundred and twelve. More than anyone in the index but himself.

The Department has a young man who has written twice, very civilly, to ask what arrangements Dr Spilsbury would wish to make for his papers. Dr Spilsbury has not yet replied. There are things in these boxes that it would be better, for a number of people, if they were not in them. He knows exactly which things, and where. He has had a fire laid in July for the purpose.

He takes out the notebook for 1946, and opens it, and begins.

## 1946–1947

*6 Apr. R. Tar, 26. Eton (King's Scholar), Trinity (scholar), Portland for the duration: the shrimp man. Father I.C.S., Bengal; mother with him; boy sent home at six, holidays with an aunt at Tunbridge Wells. (From his file. He would not have thought to tell me; he does not know it is a thing to tell.) Has not, I think, been praised by a grown man in his life. It is in the shoulders. Took him to Partridge's for a notebook. He said "Thank you, sir" as though I had given him a kidney.*

He remembers the shop. The smell of ink and sawdust; the boy holding the notebook in both hands and not putting it away, as if it might be taken back. He had known then. Some men one has to work at for months. With Reginald it was there the first morning, in the shoulders and the hands and the *sir*.

*29 Apr. Names. R. found them alone, in eleven nights, from noise. Very good ears; told him so. He did not know what to do with his face. Note for future use.*

He reads that twice. It is quite true. He has used it -- he runs his finger down the margin, where he has kept the tally in pencil -- four times since. It has worked every time.

*13 Feb 47. R. ill (influenza; 102°). Oranges (4), from the second steward of the Jaffa boat, for an opinion on the steward's brother-in-law's liver. Value received. Hand on his forehead: he shut his eyes. No one has done that for him in years, if ever; I am nearly sure of it. Read him the Lancet. He asked after Nine before he asked after himself.*

He remembers that room too. The window furred white; the boy under two overcoats, asleep at last with one hand on the paper bag. He sat on afterwards with the *Lancet* open on his knee for an hour and ten minutes by his watch, doing nothing whatever. It is not in the notebook. He has never been able to account for it. Nor has he ever tried, which is not like him. He has noticed that too, more than once, and has not tried to account for that either.

*10–14 Apr. Eleven. (Sections: box 33.) Heart as suspected. R. on the hydrophone throughout, next door. Third hour, I looked in. He said, "Would you like to hear?" I would. A very beautiful recording. -- He has crossed over. Pleased.*

*Note: she made the one call for nine hours on the first day. Type D, R. says: alarm. One wonders to whom.*

He looks at *one wonders* for some time. He did not wonder. He knew perfectly well to whom, then and now: to no one; to the far side of two concrete walls; to whatever she had been calling before she was brought up the river. He wrote *one wonders* because it is the sort of thing a man writes. He remembers that he took the carnation out of his buttonhole before he went in, and put it in a glass of water on the store-cupboard windowsill, so that it would not wilt. A man should keep something fresh.

## 1948–1949

*Mar 48. Fenwick gone (hands). Told R. "He'll be back." He will not; F. is the kind that does not come back. But R. should hear me say that they do.*

*Jan 49. R. proposes 22, 24, 25, 27 together. All four sick. He calls it an experiment. It is hope. Permitted: he had better learn now what hope costs. It will be cheaper than learning it later.*

*3 Feb. Negative, all four. Told him I was very pleased with him -- true. He sat afterwards and wrote, and then looked at what he had written for eleven minutes by the clock over the boilers. He will resign tomorrow, or the day after.*

He turns the page, and there it is, the same evening, in his own fine hand:

*Hyp. A: R. wants the work.*

*Hyp. B: R. wants me to want him to do the work.*

*NB. A and B predict identical behaviour on all measures to date. Discriminating test: let him go. If A, he will find work elsewhere and stay at it. If B, he will come back.*

It is a good design. He was pleased with it at the time. He finds himself wondering now, idly, whether Reginald ever learned to set out a problem like that -- two hypotheses, and the test between them -- and supposes that he must have. He taught him everything else.

*5 Feb. Resigned. Has kept everything; said so plainly, as though it were a threat. It is one. Very good. Proud of him; told him that too, which was true and useful, the best kind of thing to say.*

*His paper. Struck "rudimentary language" and gave him "none of it rises to" instead. He is right, of course. If they have a language, someone will ask whether they are people, and the answer is yes, and then where should we all be? He will not forgive me for some years. Then he will understand that I have saved him a great deal of trouble.*

*Let him go.*

He reads *the answer is yes* without any particular feeling. It was true in 1949; it is true now. He has never seen the use of pretending otherwise to himself. That is what notebooks are for.

## 1950–1953

*Oct 50. Postcard to R., Plymouth: "Barnacles?" No reply. (A.)*

*Apr 51. R. to the West Indies, to an institute for the species. A colonel's wife with an institute, as other colonels' wives have a rose garden. He will be bored inside the year. (B.)*

*May 53. Fleming at the club, about a young man at the Bevans' place and Beta: "a sort of mental connection." He had it from a young friend out there who draws. To the Martin with Ferris.*

*June 53. Beta gone in the night, and the boy with him. Tar's hands: sand in the abrasions, under four hours' rope. Twelve hours, or a little more. The doctor said "Ten" -- at once, too loud. A sentimental man, a veterinary training (cf. F.), a dreadful liar. Of no consequence. I find him touching.*

*Praised R.'s paper. It went in; for a second he was twenty-six. Then he closed. He has learned to close. (From me.)*

*R. stood up straight when I said his name, and then let his shoulders down again one at a time, thinking I would not see.*

*Mrs Bevan: gloves to shake hands, in that heat, and again at the pens, and would not take them off. A colonial habit, or a complaint of the skin. Some women are like that about their hands.*

He turns that page without stopping.

*Offered R. the berth. He will come on Thursday. (B.)*

*Next day. Gamma and Delta together, before Ferris. Told him they would have each other's throats out, and believed it. They did not. Pobil knelt in the slop and said "No teeth"; and R. -- this is the thing -- R. looked at him to be told what he was seeing. He has never looked at anyone for that but me.*

*Error: Pobil. Not "of no consequence." The reverse.*

*Why did they not kill each other? Cf. '49: all four sick. This one came off Ferris's boat this morning as sound as anything I have seen in ten years. Hyp.: it is the sickness, not the company. Untested. Not my animals. One might tell R. One will not; let him find it.*

*Ferris will not have Tar moved. Very well. (A, it seems. For now.)*

*Thursday. Pobil said "Please don't" about the sections. Liked him for it. On the jetty told R. he had the best ears of any of them, which is true, and to keep listening, which he will. Told Pobil to stay with the dead. He will not; he is not that kind.*

*Asked young Moss to send me reports. Particularly the domestic arrangements; young men never think those signify.*

*Nov 53. Moss: "Mr Tar's hut always very tidy. Hardly slept in." So.*

He remembers reading Moss's letter in this chair. He remembers that he laughed out loud, alone in the room, which he does not often do; and that it was not, entirely, the laugh of a man who has found something useful.

He remembers, too, what he did afterwards, which was nothing in particular. He poured himself a second whisky, which he does not often do either, and sat on with Moss's letter on his knee. The house was very quiet. Mrs Dawes had gone up. On the floor above there were three bedrooms, all made up, all very tidy; he has always kept a tidy house.

He found that he was thinking about a back bedroom in Greenwich with the window furred white on the inside, and a young man asleep in it under two overcoats with one hand on a paper bag; and the thought came to him, unasked, that the boy was presumably not cold now. He did not examine it. He thought instead about the chair across the hearth from this one, which Mrs Dawes dusts on Thursdays. And about the doctor on the jetty, saying "Ten" too fast and too loud, for no reason that could possibly do him any good; and he tried, as an exercise, to recall a single occasion on which anyone had told a lie of that kind on his behalf -- badly, at once, without stopping to work out whether it was wise -- and could not. A great many people, over forty years, have lied to him very well.

It was nearer to -- but he does not finish the thought. It is not in the notebook.

## 1955

*Feb 55. Harley St. Six months, he says. He is a kind man; I should have said four.*

*Mar. Corinne's boats: a camp, forty miles up. A tin. R.T. on the cap; Leslie's hand on the chart. The boy alive, and writing. I should very much like to see R.'s face.*

*The boy's pages. Good observation, poor spelling. "It IS a language." Yes. "Tell H. that they still say his name," and a call written out. H.: a predecessor at the Martin? A Hollis, a Hughes? A man, by the pronoun -- no: "his" is the dead one; H. is whoever is left. Moss knows of no H. Find out.*

He never did find out. He frowns at the entry. It is the only loose end in the 1955 notebook, and he dislikes loose ends; and he finds that he cannot, even now, think of anyone at the Martin whose name begins with H.

*Apr. To the Martin. R. had a nod from me, and minded it. Good.*

*Pobil will go into the water. He is a man who goes into water. Take him. I want to know what he sees.*

*(I might go in myself. To what end? The faculty is in the one who looks, not in the one who offers his face, and the boy has no reason to look at me. And I am not at present a strong swimmer.)*

He reads the parenthesis twice. It is all perfectly true.

*Corinne, second day. R. at the chart table on sound in fresh water over salt: twenty minutes, exactly as in '46. Then he saw Pobil at the companionway and stopped. Stopped for him. He never once stopped for me.*

*Third night. Told Pobil about the fingers; do not know why. Asked him how they manage without it. "Badly. Out loud. In two languages." Said, "How very inefficient." Then said, "Still." Do not know why I said that either.*

He finds that he has his pen in his hand. He looks at *Still* for a long time. He could strike it. He has never struck anything out in his life.

He puts the pen down.

*River. R. played the lure; Beta answered; and R.'s face -- I have not seen that face since Names. Pobil went over the side. Put his hand to the boy's face. From the stern I could see Pobil's. Not the boy's.*

*Then R. played them Eleven. I knew her at once. He kept her. Of course he kept her; I taught him to. (Not A. Not B.)*

*Pobil went into the river as though there were nothing in him he minded being seen. I do not think I have met such a man before. I am not certain I believe in him.*

*R. lets him see everything; I watched it at the chart table. R. never let me see one thing he had not chosen to show me. When did that begin? After '53. After me. Because of me? If so, it is a better result than any I had at Deptford.*

*Cabin. Pobil: "Like being seen. That's all. He saw me. It took about a second. Then he turned his face away."*

*I said thank you.*

*Gave him the tin. Wanted nothing for it; had already had it.*

*Sent R. "very neatly done," by Pobil. He will pretend not to care. He will not manage it.*

*The cay. Did not go ashore. R. stopped at the head of the ladder and looked at me -- at the body, at last; nine years. Saw him see it. Told him that was how it was done. He said, "Goodbye, sir." He did not say it in 1949.*

*Shut my eyes. Listened to the launch go in.*

*Hyp. C:*

And there it stops. The rest of the page is ruled and empty.

He looks at it for a long time. He has never in his life been unable to finish a sentence in a notebook.

## Entire

The fire has burnt low. Mrs Dawes comes in to make it up, and he lifts a hand, and she goes away again.

He knows exactly what is in the twelve notebooks on his knee and at his feet, and what it would do in the wrong hands, and in the right ones. The tin. The hut. *Ten.* Eleven on the second tape. A boy alive in a river the charts don't name. Moss's letter. A colonel's wife's gloves, though that is of no importance. If he put the twelve on the fire, Reginald would be safe from them for good, and Pobil, and Miss Leslie, and whoever H. may be. It would cost nothing at all.

It would get him nothing, either. He has never in his life done a thing that did not get him something he wanted. He sits with the 1955 notebook open at *Hyp. C*, and tries -- for perhaps the first time, he thinks, though he would not care to swear to it -- to work out what that would be.

He thinks of the stern of the launch, and the brown water, and Pobil's face, which he could see, and the boy's, which he could not. He thinks that the boy was thirty yards off, and that he is not at present a strong swimmer. He thinks that the faculty is in the one who looks. He thinks, quite clearly, *I could have gone in* -- and then something in him moves, quick and smooth, the way a good hand moves a blade aside from a vessel it does not mean to open, and he is thinking instead about the Department, and its civil young man, and the arrangements.

He picks up his pen and writes to the young man. It is a short letter.

*My papers are to be deposited entire.*

Then he closes the notebook on *Hyp. C*, and puts it in its box with the others, and rings for Mrs Dawes.

She carries the boxes back up one at a time. When the last of them is gone she comes down again to see to the fire, and stops beside his chair; and, without asking, the way one does with an invalid, she puts the back of her hand to his forehead.

It is cool and dry, and stays there a moment. He shuts his eyes.

He does not think about it any further, because there is no reason to.
