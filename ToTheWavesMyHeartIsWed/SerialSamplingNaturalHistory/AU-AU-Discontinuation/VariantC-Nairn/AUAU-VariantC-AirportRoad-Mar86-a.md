# The Airport Road

## I — the same in both

Nairn went out at eight in the hotel car with both cases and the box of offprints, an hour and
forty minutes before he needed to, because he cannot stand aeroplanes and has to be in the
building a long while before he gets into one and would tell you so himself if you asked him.

So it was Ramón's car, and Reggie in the front of it with his hat on his knees, and the road out
past the lagoon at twenty past nine on the Friday morning.

Ramón had not slept. He had gone to bed at half past one with the whole of it in his hands and
had lain there until four getting nowhere, and had got up and sat in the kitchen until six.

---

The first ten minutes were fine. They were about the pharmacy again, because the man picks a
thing up where he put it down, and he wanted to know about the licensing changes in 1977 and
whether the wholesalers had come out of it better or worse, and Ramón told him, and he asked
three more.

Then there was a stretch of road with the water on the left and nothing on the right and Ramón
took both hands off the wheel to shift his grip on it and put them back.

"I'm going to ask you something I've no business asking," he said. "And here's the thing, and I
want it said before I ask it, because I've been thinking about it since one o'clock this morning
and it's the part that's wrong.

"You'll answer it. You'll answer it because that's how you're made, and you'll not tell me to
mind my own business, and you'll not be angry about it later. Sixteen years I've had that man in
my house and he's the same. So it's not fair, what I'm about to do, and I know it isn't, and I'm
doing it anyway. That's the whole of my apology and it isn't much of one."

Reggie turned his hat round once on his knees.

"That is correct and I do not mind," he said. "I would rather be asked than have somebody sit
next to me deciding not to. Ask it."

And Ramón asked it, and it took him about two seconds, and he had been building it since one in
the morning.

"Do you want him back?"

---

# Version A — *yes*

## II

"Yes," said Reggie.

The road went on. Ramón kept his eyes where they were and did not say anything, because he has
stood behind a counter for forty-eight years and knows what a man sounds like when he has more.

"I should like to be exact, because the word *back* is doing a good deal of work and I do not
think it means to you what it means when it is examined. May I take it in parts."

"Take as long as you like. It's twenty minutes."

"Then. If you are asking whether I want the arrangement of 1954 to 1966 restored, the answer is
no, and it is categorical, and I would refuse it if it were offered tomorrow and I should refuse
it inside a second. I know now what that arrangement was and what it did to the man running it,
and there is no version of it that does not do it, because the thing that did the damage was the
part that made it work. I would not put a man in it. I would very particularly not put him in
it."

"All right."

"If you are asking whether I have stopped wanting him: no. I have not. I have checked it four
times, which I mention because you may want to know that it is a result and not a mood. In 1970,
when he wrote to say he had found you. In 1977. In the October of 1983. And last night at about
eleven o'clock in the taxi. It has returned the same result on every occasion and I have no
reason whatever to expect it to return a different one, and I am sixty-nine and I have stopped
waiting for it to."

Ramón found the road had gone slightly odd on him and slowed down.

"There is a third thing and I want it in the answer because without it the answer is misleading.
It has never once competed with anything.

"That is not a courtesy to you and it is not a courtesy to Nairn, and I would not offer either
of you a courtesy in place of a fact. It is a statement about the mechanism. I do not run
comparisons. I have never been able to; I have not the equipment for it and I have known that
about myself for a very long time. The thing sits where it is. It does not move, it does not ask
for anything, it has never asked for anything, and it has not cost that man one hour in nine
years."

"That's not a want, where I come from."

"No. I am aware that most people would say a thing which never asks for anything is not a want,
and I have thought about it a good deal and I do not believe they are correct, and I concede
that I may be the wrong person to adjudicate the question." He was looking out at the water.
"But I should not like you to go away with the idea that there is a man in Trinidad sitting in a
room being unhappy about your husband. There is not. There is a fact in a drawer."

---

Ramón drove about half a mile.

"Does he know?"

"No."

"Would you tell him? If he asked you."

There was a pause, and it was not a long one, and Ramón has thought about the length of it since.

"He will not ask."

"That's not what I asked you."

"No. It is not." Reggie put his hat straight on his knees. "Yes. I answer questions."

---

## III

He handed him out at the kerb and shook his hand and said the things you say, and Reggie thanked
him for the driving and for Thursday and for the dominoes he had not been at, and said that he
had enjoyed the week, and Ramón believed him.

Then he went in through the doors to find the Scotchman, who had been in the building since half
past eight and would have picked out the seats.

Ramón sat in the car for a bit before he started it.

---

He drove home the long way round the lagoon and this is what he had.

Twenty years, and the whole of it has been sitting four thousand miles away in a drawer, and it
is available to Pascual for the price of one question, and it always has been, and Pascual will
never ask it. Not because he is proud and not because he is frightened. Because he answers
questions and does not put them, and because putting that one would be a report on his own
condition, which is the single thing that man cannot do; and Ramón has known that about him
since about 1974 and never had a use for it before.

Two men, four thousand miles apart, each of them entirely willing to say the whole of it, and a
question between them that neither one can be the first to put.

He had seen it at a dinner table on the Tuesday and had thought it was about the room.

---

He has not told Pascual and he is not going to, and he has two reasons and only one of them
does him credit.

The first is that it would put a man of seventy-four in front of a choice that does not exist.
Nothing is on offer. Reggie would refuse the arrangement and has said so, and the wanting asks
for nothing and never has, and all Pascual would get out of it is the knowledge that it is
there — which he would then have in his hands for the rest of his life with no more use for it
than Ramón has.

The second is that Ramón is not going to hand his own husband a reason to look at a door, at
seventy-three, after sixteen years, and he is not going to pretend to himself that this is not
part of it. He has been over that in the kitchen more than once and it has not improved.

So it sits there, alongside the other one he has never said, and that is two now, in sixteen
years, which he had always thought was a better score than most men manage.

He put the kettle on when he got in. Pascual asked whether the flight had gone and Ramón said it
had, and asked what he wanted for his lunch.

---

# Version B — *no*

## II

"No," said Reggie.

Ramón took that on the chin and drove for a few seconds, and then Reggie said:

"I want to be exact, because that is a short answer to a large question and it will be
misunderstood if I leave it there. May I take it in parts."

"Go on."

"I wanted it back. I can give you the dates, because I can give anybody the dates. From the
ninth of November 1966 continuously until some time in 1980, and I did not regard that as a
difficulty and did not report it to anybody, because there was nobody to report it to and
nothing whatever to be done about it. Fourteen years. I want that in the answer, because if I
say no without it I have told you something untrue about most of my life."

"All right."

"It stopped in 1980 and I can tell you how, and it was not a matter of getting over anything. I
do not get over things. It was that I found out what the object was.

"For fourteen years I had taken it that what I wanted back was a man. In fact what I wanted back
was a condition — the condition of being known by somebody without having to say anything, which
I cannot produce and have never been able to produce, and which I had had for twelve years from
one person and had therefore never seen apart from that person. I had no way to separate them.
They came together and I assumed they were the same object, which is exactly the error you make
when you have an n of one and I have spent my life telling other people so.

"In 1980 I had the condition again, by an entirely different route, from somebody who does not
do what he does and could not do it. Which separated them. And when the condition was subtracted
there was nothing underneath it that was asking for anything at all."

"And you checked it."

"Twice. In the October of 1983 and again in the December. Both times the same. And last night in
the taxi, which is three, and which I did not intend to do and did anyway."

---

Ramón drove about half a mile.

"So what's left?"

"A great deal, and I do not want you to take the answer as less than it is." He was looking out
at the water. "I am extremely fond of him. I would do a very considerable amount for him and I
have never had occasion to find out how much. I want the correspondence to go on and I should
mind losing it more than I am able to put a figure on, and I have never been able to put a
figure on it, which is unusual for me and I have noticed it.

"And I do not want him back. Those are not in conflict. I am aware that they sound as though
they are, and I am aware that most people would hear the first three and conclude the fourth,
and they would be wrong, and I have checked."

---

"Does he know?"

"No."

"Would you tell him? If he asked you."

There was a pause, and it was not a long one, and Ramón has thought about the length of it since.

"He will not ask."

"That's not what I asked you."

"No. It is not." Reggie put his hat straight on his knees. "Yes. I answer questions."

---

## III

He handed him out at the kerb and shook his hand and said the things you say, and Reggie thanked
him for the driving and for Thursday and for the dominoes he had not been at, and said that he
had enjoyed the week, and Ramón believed him.

Then he went in through the doors to find the Scotchman, who had been in the building since half
past eight and would have picked out the seats.

Ramón sat in the car for a good while before he started it.

---

He drove home the long way round the lagoon and this is what he had.

He had, in his hands, in a car, the one piece of information that would have taken twenty years
off a man of seventy-four, and it was the wrong shape.

*He is not waiting for you.* That is a kindness. It is the kindness, it is the whole of the
kindness, and Pascual has never once asked for it and has never once said aloud that he wanted
it. And the trouble with a kindness of that shape is that when you have given it there is
nothing standing where the thing used to be. Sixteen years his man has been carrying something
he has never named and never put down. Ramón would be taking it off him and handing back an
empty pair of hands, and telling him, at the same moment, that the carrying was never joined to
anything at the other end.

Fourteen years, though. That is the part that would not leave him alone at the lagoon. Fourteen
years it *was* joined at the other end, exactly, on both sides at once, and neither of them knew,
and neither of them could have found out without one of them putting a question that neither of
them can put. And by the time anybody could have told either of them, it had gone on one side
and not the other.

He had seen it at a dinner table on the Tuesday and had thought it was about the room.

---

He has not told him. He came the closest he has come in sixteen years, twice, and both times he
was on the point of it and both times he stopped, and he is not certain he was right and does
not expect ever to be certain.

What he will say for himself is this: it is not his. It is a thing a man told him in a car on
the way to an aeroplane, in answer to a question that man had no obligation to answer and
answered because he cannot do otherwise, and it was not offered to be carried anywhere. If it is
to go across that water it wants to go from the one to the other, and there is a question that
would fetch it, and it costs five words, and Ramón has been in the same house as the man who
would have to say them for sixteen years and cannot say them for him.

So it sits there, alongside the other one he has never said, and that is two now, in sixteen
years, which he had always thought was a better score than most men manage.

He put the kettle on when he got in. Pascual asked whether the flight had gone and Ramón said it
had, and asked what he wanted for his lunch.
