# Unblinding

## I

She heard on the twenty-eighth of November, three weeks after, from a man in Port of Spain who
copied her in on a circular because her name was still on a list from 1956.

She wrote to San Juan that evening. It took her about forty minutes and she has no criticism of it
now: the date, the hour, the place, the fact that it was quick, who came, what was said. She did
not put in a single word of comfort, on the grounds that there wasn't one and that Pascual would
know it and would be spared having to receive it politely.

She did not put in April 1984 either.

She had a reason at the time and it was that the man had just been told and you don't hand
somebody a second thing on the same day. It's a decent reason and she believed it, and she has
since observed that it has the same property as the reason she gave herself on the Thursday in
April, which is that it arrived complete and she did not go and look at what it was resting on.

## II

His letter came in the March, and it asked for two things, and the second one stopped her.

The letters she'd expected. He was entitled to them and she'd have sent them without being asked
if she'd thought of it.

He asked whether there had been a post-mortem, and whether a copy could be got.

She got it. It took six weeks through the Colonel's successor, who is a decent man and asked no
questions and did the necessary in writing. It came to her in Aberdeen in a buff envelope on the
ninth of June and she opened it at the kitchen table because there was nowhere else she was going
to open it.

She read the whole of it. She wants that in, because she could have forwarded it sealed, and there
was a moment on the ninth of June when she considered it, and what she decided was that if she was
going to post a document like that to a man of seventy-five she was going to know what was in it
first.

That is also a decent reason. She's since worked out that she read it because she wanted to know,
which is different, and that she was entitled to want to know, and that the two things being both
true does not make them the same thing.

---

The technical part she read twice and it is not the part.

*The body is that of a poorly-nourished male, height 167 cm, weight 48.6 kg.*

She got a pencil and did it in stones because she can't think in kilogrammes about a man she used
to sit across a bench from, and it came to seven stone nine, and she put the pencil down and sat
there.

She has one figure of her own and it isn't much good. Between 1954 and 1957 he was a compactly
built man who did not look like anything in particular and whom she'd have put at about nine and a
half stone if anybody had asked her, and nobody did, and she has no idea what he weighed in 1970 or
1975 or last year, because she never saw him again after the fifth of April 1957.

Nine and a half to seven nine, at a rate of nothing at all per year, over a period in which she
wrote to him perhaps thirty times about work and got thirty replies, all of them lucid, all of
them in that hand.

There was no year in which anybody could have seen it. She has established that to her own
satisfaction and it does not help, because there was one man who did not need to see it and had
asked her in the April of 1984 for a reading, and she had given him a reading.

## III

She tried three times to write something to go with it and in the end she sent a compliments slip
with nothing on it.

The three attempts are gone. She remembers what killed each of them, which is that every one of
them had to begin somewhere, and the only place any of them could begin was the April of 1984, and
there is no sentence that delivers the April of 1984 to that man without also delivering what it
cost him — because he'd get there in under a minute, and he'd get there on his own, and the letter
would then have been a device for making him do it.

So she sent it blank, and she knew exactly how a blank slip would read to a man who had spent
nineteen years being handled, and she sent it anyway, and she told herself it was the least
intrusive available option.

It was the least intrusive available option. It was also the third time in twenty-six months she
had produced a complete and sound reason for not saying something, and by the June she had noticed
that this was a series.

## IV

She wrote to him on the fourteenth of September, and it is four sides, and it is the only letter
she has ever written that she made a copy of.

She told him about the April of 1984. All of it and in order: the blinding and how well it was
done, the sixty-seven, the hour and a half at the table, the three features and which one he'd seen
and which one he'd missed.

She told him the figure she gave, and then she told him the figure she held, and she did not
soften the gap between them or explain it away. She wrote that she had been asked for a reading of
a text and had given one, correctly, and that a correct answer to the question asked is not the
same as the most useful thing she had, and that she had known that at the time.

She told him she had got out the San Juan address on the Thursday and drafted four lines, and she
copied the four lines out for him so that he could see what he had not been sent.

She told him she had noticed, on the day, that her reason had arrived too fast, and had recorded
the observation and not acted on it.

And she told him what she thinks the reason was resting on, which she did not have in April 1984
and has had since about the June:

That she had concluded, in the October of 1966, that Pascual Ortega was a man who would not act;
that she had put that conclusion in writing and posted it to him; that she had never once in
nineteen years tested it, revised it, or had occasion to; and that when a moment came in the spring
of 1984 in which acting on it or not acting on it made a material difference, she consulted a
nineteen-year-old finding about a man she had not seen since 1957 and treated it as current.

She wrote: *I did not decline to write to you because of method. Method was what I reached for
afterwards. I declined to write to you because I had decided in 1966 what you were, and I was
comfortable, and I had never had to look at it since.*

And then, because she is what she is and there was no version of the letter that left it out:

*I do not withdraw the 1966 letter. You went, and it could not be retracted, and I have never
thought otherwise for one day. What I withdraw is the use I made of it afterwards, which was to
stop looking at you.*

## V

His reply came in the October and it is nine lines.

He thanked her for the post-mortem and for going to the trouble of the Colonel's successor. He
said that he had done the arithmetic on the weight himself and had a 1969 figure to work from and
that his own came out at two stone, and that he thought hers was the better estimate because it had
more of the interval in it.

Then he wrote:

*You are right about everything except the finding.*

*I said it. On the fourteenth — no. On the Tuesday before I went, in the room, out loud, to him. He
said it first and I confirmed it and I did not take it back. Three weeks before you wrote to me.*

*I never answered your letter because the answer was that I had done the thing you said I had never
done, and had gone anyway, and that is worse than the charge and I could not see the use of it.*

*I am sorry. It has cost more than I thought it would.*

## VI

She read it in the hall and then again in the kitchen and then she went out and walked for about
two hours in weather that did not suit it.

She has made one inference about another person's interior in her professional life, in the sense
of a stated finding under her own name that she was prepared to be held to, and she made it in the
October of 1966 in four pages, and she was wrong, and she was wrong on the day she posted it.

She has thought about the symmetry more than is useful. Reggie Tar made one in fifty years and it
was correct and it cost him the man. She made one in fifty-nine and it was false and it stood for
twenty years and nobody corrected it because the only person who could would have had to convict
himself to do it.

She has also worked out, and would rather not have, that a great deal follows from it that she
cannot untangle: she does not know whether the April of 1984 would have gone differently if the
finding had been retracted in 1967. She would like to say yes and cannot support it. Method was a
real reason as well as a convenient one. Both things being true is the whole of the difficulty and
it does not resolve.

She wrote back in the November, and it was short, and she did not tell him it was all right,
because it was not and he would have known.

She wrote that the retraction was accepted and entered, that he had answered a letter within twenty
years which is well inside the interval for that class of correspondence, and that she had one
question if he would answer it.

*Did he ever get the figure? Not the blood pressure. The one you should have asked for.*

She knew the answer. She asked because the question is a question with a number for an answer, and
she has known where that method comes from since about 1955, and there is nobody left in the world
to ask it of except him.
