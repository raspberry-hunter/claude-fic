# Baseline — kiss

"You'll *revise* it," Pascual says, and laughs, once, terribly, and then he has Reggie by the front of the shirt and is not laughing.

The first observation is that he knows precisely what this is.

That is not nothing. He has a baseline. He has fifteen months of one, taken twice a week and sometimes three, at close quarters, by an observer who was not bad even then, and there is very little about the operation for which he does not hold a figure. He knows what happens and in what order and what it does and how long it lasts and what it is worth against the third glass. He set it down once, in a February, in three words, and did not join it to anything, and the three words were *not unpleasant*, and it was an accurate measurement, and he has carried it about for six years as the whole of what there was to know.

The second observation is that it does not transfer.

Not that this is more. More is a quantity and a quantity can be handled; he is good with quantities. This is the same operation performed on him, near enough — within the variance of what the operation admits of, certainly — and it is not the same thing at all, and there is no unit in which the two of them can be set down on one sheet. The old figures are not wrong. They are about something else, and he does not know what, and he is not going to find out standing up.

He makes both of those somewhere near the desk with his glasses gone askew and Pascual's mouth on his and Pascual's hand flat and hard between his shoulder blades, holding him in as though he might otherwise go somewhere.

The third observation is that he has got both hands in Pascual's hair and did not decide to.

He put his hands on somebody often enough in the fifteen months, and in a good many ways. He was asked to, in terms, by a man who was not shy of asking for things and who liked to be specific about them, and he complied, accurately, and did it well, because he does most things well. What he did not do once in fifteen months is put a hand anywhere unasked. Not once. He was accurate about that at the time and thought nothing of it, because there was no item that called for it and the question did not arise.

There is one uninstructed act in the whole of that file — a Monday night in the March of 1947, going down a hill nobody had told him to go down, for a purpose he was not afterwards able to state — and it does not come to him now, and will not for a very long time.

Pascual breathes out when he does it, and shifts his grip, and comes in harder.

The fourth observation is that he would like that to happen again.

Not the grip. The breathing out. He would like to be the cause of it a second time, and he is already, somewhere well below the level at which he is conducting any of this, working out what he did and whether it will bear repeating.

That is not an observation. He has made a great many observations about what other people do and not one of them has ever been a want, and there is no procedure for a want; it cannot be ruled off, it is not a finding, it is not even about him. It will not go anywhere on the sheet. It is the truest thing he has had all evening and he has it in the middle of a room he has stopped being able to describe.

The fifth is that the sound in the room is his own, and that this brings the evening's rate to two, and that a rate of two is not one he has ever run.

He does not get as far as a sixth. There is a stretch of some minutes from which he subsequently has no observations about himself at all.

He has a great many about Pascual. They are good ones and they are in order and he could write them up on the Tuesday, and one or two of them he will still have in thirty years. It is the other column that is empty, and its being empty has not happened to him before. It did not happen once in fifteen months, in a room where it might reasonably have been expected to and where he had every encouragement; he kept both columns throughout, on every occasion, without interruption, and could have written the whole of it up the following morning, and on two occasions did.

Then he is sitting on the edge of the desk with his shirt open and Pascual standing between his knees with both hands on his face, breathing like a man who has run up a hill.

"No," Pascual says.

Reggie's hand is shut in the front of Pascual's shirt. It does not open when it is instructed to and there is an interval, not a long one, in which the instruction has to be given again.

He is not at this moment capable of the sentence, but it assembles anyway, because it always does.

"That is not consistent with the last four minutes."

It comes out at the wrong pitch. He hears it go, and hears what is in it, and there is no operation available for calling it back.

"I don't put a needle in a compromised animal," Pascual says. "I've said it to you about Beta, I said it to you in front of Jean, and I'm not going to make an exception of you at midnight with three whiskies in you and that boy out there on the sand. No."

"That is not a refusal."

"No," Pascual agrees. "It isn't." He takes his hands away, slowly, in the manner of a man setting down something he has been asked to be careful with, and steps back, and picks Reggie's glasses up off the floor.

He puts them into Reggie's hand and the hand is not steady enough to take them, and he sees that, and closes Reggie's fingers round them with his own, and does not say anything about it.

"Tomorrow you'll be sober and I'll still be standing a foot too close. Then we'll see what your eight observations were worth."

Then he goes out, and Reggie hears the tap, and Pascual comes back with a glass of water and stands over him while he drinks the whole of it, exactly as he stands over Charles at mealtimes, and it is at that point that Reggie has to look away.

Pascual takes the chair.

"You are not staying."

"I'm staying," Pascual says, "because I'm not going down to the pens before breakfast, and I'd rather be somewhere I can see the man who asked me not to."

Reggie lies down on the bed in his clothes. The generator goes on. He thinks, once, with great clarity, that if the surf is running at all the boy will not manage the last twenty yards on his own and will have to get the animal to take some of it, and that this is information, and that it is of no use to anybody, and then it goes, and he sleeps.

He wakes at first light with a blanket over him that he did not put there. Pascual is asleep in the chair with his arms folded and his chin down, and looks, in that position, about sixty.

Reggie lies still for a while.

Then he gets up, quietly, and does not go to the window, and does not go out. The fourth glass is standing where Pascual left it, out of reach, and he drinks it off warm because it will otherwise be wasted, and it tastes of nothing at all.

He takes a clean sheet from the desk and writes on the corner of it, *3 — insufficient*, and underlines it, and puts the sheet in the drawer with the mortality tables.

Then he sits down again with the pen still in his hand, because there is a second figure from last night and it wants recording.

He has it, and he got it by subtraction, which is the one operation still open to a man who was not taking readings. He knows what the clock said when Pascual took hold of his shirt and he knows what it said when he was sitting on the desk with his shirt open, and the difference is six minutes.

He said four. He said it out loud, to Pascual, in the room, in a sentence he was rather pleased with at the time.

He has never in his working life been out on an interval by half as much again. He has estimated durations under fire, in a wheelhouse, in the dark, with a stopwatch in the other hand and no complaint from anybody, and last night he was standing still in a lit room and lost a third of it, and of the six he can give an account of about two.

He writes the numeral and stops.

Because a figure goes under a heading. He has kept records for nineteen years and he has no heading for this one, and the only thing in the file that is even the same class of measurement is three words from a February, and they will not do. He knows they will not do. They were accurate and they were a measurement of something else, and there is no arithmetic anywhere that gets from *not unpleasant* to a third of an interval missing, and he sits over the sheet at ten to six in the morning trying to make the difference between them smaller than it is.

He puts the pen down and goes out.
