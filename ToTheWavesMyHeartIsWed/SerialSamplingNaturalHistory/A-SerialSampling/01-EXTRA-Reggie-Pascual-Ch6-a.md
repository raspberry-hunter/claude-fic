Pascual is wet to the shoulder and has not sat down since five o'clock, and when Reggie says that he would like a word, he does not move.

"I told the boy I would stay with the animal."

"Ten minutes."

"You may have your ten minutes here."

Beta has come up to the near side of the pen and is lying at the surface with his eyes half shut, doing nothing at all, in a manner Reggie has not seen him do at any point in nineteen weeks.

"I would rather not," Reggie says.

"Why?"

"Because I do not know what it can hear."

Pascual makes a short sound that is not a laugh. But he comes, as far as the generator housing, and stands with his arms folded and the sun coming up behind him over the neck of the cay, and looks at Reggie the way a man looks at a horse he has been told is sound.

"If you are going to ask me for another week --"

"No."

"Then what is it you want?"

"I meant to stop it last night," Reggie says.

Pascual is silent for a moment. "This morning you said he should go back to Gamma."

"Yes."

"Which of those am I to believe?"

"Both. That is the difficulty."

The generator note dips and comes back. Reggie is aware that he is blinking, and that Pascual has noticed him blinking, and that neither of these facts is any use to him.

"I am going to tell you the whole of it," he says. "It will take longer than ten minutes and you will not like any part of it. I should like to do it in order."

"Then do it in order."

"I had the hypothesis in April. Not in June, when Jean did her arithmetic on the verandah, and not last month, when you told me that you expected it was Charles. April. I had it off the logs."

"The food logs."

"The behaviour column. He wrote *No change* in it every day for eleven weeks. That is not an observation. Nobody keeps an animal for eleven weeks and has nothing whatever to enter, and he is the most conscientious record-keeper on this station, and he was writing me a blank page every day in a good clear hand." Reggie finds that he is looking at the concrete rather than at Pascual, and corrects it. "So I knew there was something he was keeping off the sheet, and I knew he intended to go on keeping it off the sheet, and I did not ask him about it."

"Why not?"

"Because a variable that is being concealed is worth more than one that has been declared. A man who has declared it starts managing it for you. He starts giving you the answer he thinks you want. Whereas he was running it clean and honest for me for nothing, and writing it down, and lying only in the one column, and I could see the shape of it in the other three." He hears the sentence arrive. "That is what I thought at the time. I am not offering it to you as anything else."

"Go on."

"The second thing. He got that animal in the first place because two of the staff were on leave and Jean and I had the new specimen. That is true and it is not the whole of it. Beta had beaten nothing at that point. Beta was expected to follow Alpha, and I expected to lose whoever was in that pen a great deal of time for no return, and possibly a hand." Reggie makes himself say it without any furniture round it. "I gave the dangerous animal to the man I could most easily do without."

Pascual's jaw moves. He does not say anything.

"The third thing is the design, and the design is the part I would still defend, and I would like you to hear all of it before you tell me what you think of it. Two withdrawals, crossed. Him off Beta, her off Gamma, and each of them onto the other. There is no version of that which permits partial contact. If I let him look at the animal for five minutes a day I have not withdrawn anything, I have only made the dose smaller and lost the power to say what the dose was. So it was total, and it had to be total, and I had him watched at night for the first nine days because he kept trying to go back, and on the ninth day he stopped trying and I stopped having him watched." He pauses. "I was pleased about that. I entered it as compliance."

"Compliance."

"He had given up. I wrote it down as compliance."

"And the sampling?"

"The sampling is yours." Reggie looks at him. "You told me in March that a single figure is worth nothing and that what I wanted was a trajectory, over days, with the same instrument and the same hand. You were entirely right and it is the best methodological point anybody has made to me since I came here. I applied it to the man."

Pascual does not move at all for a moment. Then he says, very quietly, "You are thanking me."

"I am telling you where it came from. You are entitled to know that."

"I am not entitled to it. Nobody is entitled to that." He turns half away and turns back. "Finish."

"There is one more thing before the eleventh day. In April he told me to put Gamma in with Beta. He was in no position to argue it and he argued it badly and he was right, and I knew at the time that he might be right. I told him they are territorial, that co-housing has never worked, that Beta has a history of violence and I would not risk either specimen." Reggie stops. "Every clause of that is true. None of it is why I refused."

"Then why did you refuse?"

"I do not know."

Pascual waits for the rest of it. There is no rest of it. After a while he says, "You do not know."

"I have gone over it four times and I do not have it. I should like to be able to hand you something. There is nothing there to hand you."

"And the eleventh day."

"On the eleventh day I saw him properly for the first time since it started. He was sitting by that pen with his head in his hands and he could not get a sentence out, and I had -- " He stops, because the correct word is available and he does not care for it. "I had the magnitude of it. All at once, standing in front of him. And what I did with it was suggest we go to dinner."

"And that evening I took you apart in front of everybody," Pascual says. "And I would do it again, and louder."

"Yes. I want you to understand that it worked."

"It did not work. The animal went over the wall."

"It worked on me. I went back to my room and I sat there and I did not sleep and by about three o'clock I had it settled. I was going to stop it in the morning. I had the sentence. I had worked out how to say it so that it did not look like being made to." Reggie hears how that sounds. He lets it stand, because it is accurate. "And then I came down here at seven and there was a tarp on the deck and blood on the tarp, and an animal that had dragged itself up a ladder by its arms, and a stain on the concrete the colour of port, and him asleep on a pad beside the pen with his glasses off, four hundred feet from where I had put him, in a place he could not possibly have heard anything from with this thing running." He does not point at the generator. "And I had the magnitude of it a second time, considerably larger, and it went straight through me, and what came out of my mouth was that he should go back to Gamma's pen."

"Yes," Pascual says. "I was there."

"I did not decide it. I want to be exact with you: there was no interval in which I decided it. I have gone over that too and I do not have a mechanism."

"You are describing yourself as a piece of equipment."

"I am describing what I have."

Pascual unfolds his arms. "Then I will tell you what I have. I have an animal with muscle damage that may take his kidneys in the next seventy-two hours, and I will not know until Thursday. I have a boy who could not tell me, three hours ago, whether he was all right, and who answered that question last of the four I asked him, after the animal, after the tarp and after his own bloody spectacles." He is not shouting. It is worse than the verandah. "You want to know what I think of your design. I think your design is the only thing on this station that has been properly looked after."

Reggie says nothing for a moment.

"I have been frightened since about four o'clock this morning," he says, "and I have not been able to make it stop, and I should like to tell you what of, because it is not the thing you think."

"Then tell me."

"What I have got is a result. Not a suspicion, a result: crossed withdrawal, negative both ways, a decline arrested and a decline resumed, and now this. And it is in two halves. One half is in that pen and the other half is at breakfast with Jean." He finds his voice has gone flat, which is what it does. "The people who pay for this station send a man down twice a year with a clipboard to look at our log books. They will not want to know whether it is real. They will take it that it is real and they will want to know how many more of him there are and how one goes about finding them, and they are not going to ask me, because by then I shall have been written up as a man who had it and let it walk off the premises."

"You are afraid of the Americans."

"I am afraid of the arithmetic. The Americans are only where it comes out."

Pascual is quiet. Then: "You told me in March that you left the government work. You did not tell me what it was."

"No."

"I have made a guess. I made it out loud, on the verandah, and you went white, so I take it the guess was not far off."

"It was not far off," Reggie says. "It was short."

Pascual waits.

"I cannot tell you and I am not going to. I will give you this much, and then I should be obliged if you would leave it alone. It was not done to me and I did not watch it done. It was done by me, with my hands, and I was good at it, and I have never been able to establish what I would have done if there had been a way out of the room, because there was not one, and a man who was not given the chance to refuse does not get to award himself the credit for refusing." He stops. "That has been the position for thirteen years. I should like it on record that I did not enjoy any part of it. I am aware that this is not a defence."

"It is not."

"No. It is not offered as one."

The generator dips again. Somebody down at the jetty is dragging the fish crates about, and there is a smell of coffee coming across from the mess, and Reggie discovers that he has not eaten since the middle of yesterday.

"The log," Pascual says. "What are you going to write?"

"I do not know yet."

"Do not write it."

"He has written it," Reggie says. "That is the difficulty. He has written a clean daily record for nineteen weeks in his own hand, and it is the most complete husbandry log this station possesses, and every day of it says *No change* in a column where there was a great deal of change. Anybody reading it can see the animal doing something the entry does not account for. I am the only man here who can read what is missing out of it, and I have known what is missing since April." He looks at Pascual. "It is already an exhibit. Nobody has to build anything."

"Then bury it."

"I have never once been able to sit on a result."

"Then that is what you are," Pascual says. "That is not a fault in a machine. That is a man."

Reggie considers this and finds he has nothing to put against it, which is not the same as agreeing.

"There is one more thing and then you can go back to him," he says. "I want to ask you something and I would like a straight answer, because you are the only person on this station who will give me one."

"Ask."

"If the animal had stayed in the pen. If I had come down at seven and found nothing at all -- would I have stopped it?"

Pascual looks at him for a long moment.

"How would I know?" he says. "Was I in the room with you at three o'clock in the morning? You are asking me to certify a thing that did not happen. I do not do that with an animal and I will not do it with you. There is no sample. There is one morning and it went the way it went, and you will have to live without the other one, the same as the rest of us."

"Yes," Reggie says. "Thank you."

Pascual gives him a look he cannot read, and goes back down the slope to the pen, wringing out his sleeve as he goes.

Reggie stands where he is. His hands are not steady, and he attributes this correctly to not having eaten, and makes a note to get something at the mess before the second sampling.

The stain on the concrete is drying at the edges and going brown. Somebody will hose the deck down before noon; they always do. He makes a note to ask Pascual whether he wants it photographed first.
