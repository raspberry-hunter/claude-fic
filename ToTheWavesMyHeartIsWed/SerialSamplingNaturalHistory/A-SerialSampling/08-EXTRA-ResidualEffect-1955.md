# Residual Effect

The bolts in the pen rail are set at sixteen inches on centre. He has known that since
the rail went in, in the way he knows the cycle of the generator and the fall of the
apron and the number of stairs to the laboratory, which is to say that he did not learn
it and cannot unlearn it and it is simply there when wanted.

He stands at the ninth bolt from the ladder. Pascual stands at the tenth.

Sixteen inches, then. He has it exactly, every night, and he has never once been able to
put it down.

---

The difficulty is that he cannot move.

He worked this out on the second night and it has held since, and it is the sort of thing
that is entirely sound and entirely useless. A man who has spent three months altering
conditions on another man may not afterwards alter a condition. Everything he does now
will be read. It will be read correctly, because Pascual reads him correctly and has done
for a year; and it will mean whatever it means whether or not he intended it, because
intention is not available for inspection and never has been.

So: if he moves back, that is a message. If he moves in, that is a worse one. If he moves
back and then in, that is a design. And the ninth bolt is where he was standing on the
night of the counting, before he knew he was doing anything at all, and it is therefore
the only position on the whole rail that carries no information, and he holds it the way
he used to hold the line on a bearing.

Pascual thinks he is being scrupulous. He is fairly sure of that. He would like, once, to
put it correctly — that there is nothing scrupulous about it, that a man frozen in a
doorway is not being polite — and there is no way to say it that would not itself be a
move.

---

He has the nine words. He could produce them at any hour and in his sleep; he has taken
them out perhaps four hundred times and they are not wearing.

The trouble is not the words. The trouble is the condition of use.

*When the typewriter has been going and you cannot stop.*

He can establish the typewriter. The typewriter is a fact about the world and there is a
sheet in the machine with a date on it. He cannot establish the second clause, because to
establish it he must ask himself whether he cannot stop or whether he merely wishes to go
upstairs, and the instant he asks that question he is aware of wanting the answer to come
out one way, and an observer who wants the answer to come out one way is exactly what he
was in December.

He put it to himself in the third week in its general form and did not care for the
result. The sentence is only usable by a man who does not want it to work. Any man who
wants it to work has contaminated the report by wanting it. There is no procedure that
gets round this, and Pascual knew there was not, and said so, and told him he would have
to manage.

---

He manages by asking after Delta.

He does it every day, in the morning, in the yard, in front of whoever is there. He was
told in February that he had stopped, and it was true — he had, some time in December,
and he cannot now recover the day — and so he asks, and before he asks he has to settle
whether he is asking because he wishes to know, which is permitted, or because he was
told he had stopped, which is a variable being corrected in front of the man it was run
on.

He has never settled it. Not once, in six weeks. He asks anyway, on the ground that the
condition of a receiving animal in a co-housing trial is a legitimate object of daily
enquiry irrespective of the state of the enquirer, which is a piece of reasoning he would
not accept from a second-year student.

It is the same with everything. He carries the crate up from the jetty because it is
heavy and Pascual is fifty-three; he then spends the afternoon establishing that he did
not carry it in order to be a man who carries crates. He does not mention the Copenhagen
number, which came in on the second of March and which he has in his desk, because he
cannot get it out of his desk without knowing what it is for. Civility has become
laborious in a way he could not have described in October, when it cost nothing because
he did not do any.

Twice he catches himself designing a way to be pleasant that could not be mistaken for a
condition, and stops, on the grounds that a design is what he is trying to avoid, and
that a man designing his way out of designing has not understood the objection.

---

On the ninth of March he takes four.

It works. That is the finding. Three was insufficient in August and the dose was climbing
in December and four, in March, does the whole of it: an hour of nothing usable, then
sleep, then a morning that costs him about as much as one would expect and no more.

He writes down *4* on a clean sheet at half past six in the morning, and dates it, and
then sits with the sheet in front of him for some time, because a man who records a dose
is a man who intends to titrate it, and there is no other reason on earth to write the
figure down.

He opens the drawer to file it and finds the other one, under the mortality tables, where
he put it on the thirtieth of August: *3 — insufficient*, undated, in his own hand.

He cannot destroy either of them. Destroying a record is a worse act than keeping one and
he has held that opinion since he was twenty-two and finds, to his considerable
irritation, that he still holds it. He cannot file them, because there is no heading. So
he writes the date on the August sheet, which is the only operation available to him, and
puts them both back under the tables, and goes down to breakfast and asks Pascual how
Delta is eating.

---

The nights are the arithmetic.

He does not commission it. He walks down to the ladder at half past one and by the time
he is at the rail it has been running for some minutes without being asked, and it is
always the same material: the tide on the twenty-ninth of August, the westerly set
through the passage, four hundred miles, a figure for an animal that can do a fifth of it
in a day and no figure at all for a man.

He has been adding inputs he has no right to. A fisherman at the point said something in
January about the rain in the south and he took it and used it. He knows precisely what
that is. It is not an observation, it is a man feeding his own machine until it produces
a different answer, and the machine does not produce different answers, it produces the
same one, and getting the same one is not reassurance; it is only the same one.

In the second week of March he hears a step in the corridor at about two in the morning,
outside his door, that stops and does not go on for some seconds and then goes back the
way it came. He works at it for a while and gets nothing from it and files it, provisionally,
under the generator.

He is not sleeping. He is eating, because Pascual looks at him at one o'clock and he is
not prepared to be looked at and found wanting twice; and nobody has ever watched a man
sleep, so that is where it comes out, and by the end of March he is running on about three
hours and the machinery has never been better. It is remarkable, in fact. It has not been
this good since Salisbury. He has a suspicion about that which he sets down once, in his
head, in a single sentence, and does not develop.

---

The twelfth of April.

Spilsbury's letter came on the Tuesday. The paper is first-class and there are two
questions about the eleventh of November, and they are good questions, and they are the
questions of a man who intends to go on asking. He has carried the letter about for four
days.

He answers them at the typewriter that evening between eight and twenty past ten. He
answers them honestly. Every figure is correct and every sentence is true and the whole
of it is constructed to route round the middle, which is a female specimen coming off the
bottom after four months and crossing twenty feet of water to a male she had never seen
and putting her hand flat on his face and leaving it there, and he does it well, and at
twenty past ten it is done.

And then the machinery is free.

He has not had it free since November. There has been the paper to route round, and the
routing is heavy work, and it has been taking the whole of the load; and it is done, and
there is nothing in front of the machine at all, and it does not sit idle for so much as a
minute.

It gives him six.

Six other things he could have done on the twenty-ninth of August. Fully worked, in
order, with the objections to each and the objections answered: two of them involving the
Colonel, one involving Mrs Bevan and a form of words about the Navy's own animal, one
that would have required nothing but a telegram and forty-eight hours, and one that is so
simple he has to stand up and walk to the window. He can fault none of them. He goes at
them for perhaps a quarter of an hour, hard, the way he would go at a student's design,
looking for the flaw that makes them impossible, and there is no flaw; there is only that
he did not see them on the night.

He did not see them on the night because on the night he had taken three whiskies in
order to be unable to work out where they had gone.

He sits down on the edge of the desk.

It is not that the dose cost him the course. The course came back, five days later, whole,
and he has it. It is that the dose was taken at nine o'clock and the boy did not go down
until after midnight, and there were three hours in that room in which the man who could
have produced those six things was not there, and Reggie put him out himself, deliberately,
with a measured quantity, because there was one thing he did not want to know and he was
willing to lose the rest of the apparatus to be rid of it.

He goes upstairs at about a quarter to eleven.

---

He gets half of it out.

"The typewriter has been going," he says, "and I —"

And it will not come, and he stands in the corridor with his hands at his sides finding
out why, which is not what he expected to be doing.

It will not come because it is false. He *can* stop. He has demonstrated that he can stop:
he stopped on the twenty-ninth of August, at nine in the evening, at a cost of three
glasses and six designs and a man he has no figure for. The stopping is not the difficulty.
The stopping is what he has come about. And he cannot say a false sentence to this man —
he has established that too, this month, at some length, the way one establishes anything,
by trying it and finding the mechanism will not turn — and so there is nothing at the end
of the sentence and he stands there with his jaw locked and produces nothing whatever.

Pascual stands back from the door.

"Six of nine," he says. "Come in."

He has counted them. He has stood in a doorway at that hour with a man failing in
front of him and he has *counted the words*, and produced the count, and the count is a
number.

There is no sentence in the English language that would have got Reggie through that door.
A number does it.

---

The hour before is not pleasant and he had not expected it to be. He gives an account, out
loud, of what is in the paper and what is not in it, and of what he will be thanked for,
and of what he will therefore be saying four times a year for the rest of his working life;
and the whole time he is doing it he is waiting for the reduction, because that is the
thing one is given to understand about saying a thing aloud, and there is no reduction. It
is precisely the same size at the end as it was at the beginning. He says so. He is told
that it does not, not the first six or seven times, which is a longitudinal claim about a
population of one, and he files it, hopelessly, as untestable.

Then the other thing, and the other thing is different, and he is not ready for it.

He shuts his eyes at the start, out of habit, and opens them again, and after that he
cannot get them shut.

It is the rail. That is what it is; he has the whole of it in about a second and a half.
Shutting his eyes now would be a movement, and a movement will be read, and he has been
standing at the ninth bolt for six weeks because he is not permitted to send anything, and
the paralysis has come up the stairs with him and got into a room where it has no business
whatever. So his eyes stay open. Not from courage. From the same fault that has cost him
six weeks, working normally, in a new place.

And so he is there for all of it.

What he chiefly finds is that he is a cause.

He has been an instrument since he was a child. An instrument is not in its own results;
that is the point of it; a man taking a reading is the one object in the room that does not
appear in the record. And here is a face eight inches from his own doing things, and the
things it does are consequent upon him, and he has no procedure for being the antecedent
of anything, and he can feel himself reaching for the reading and finding that he is on the
wrong side of the glass.

There is no record. That is the second finding and it takes him some time to be sure of it,
because in August there was a record and it was enormous and arrived faster than it could
be laid down and he spent the night sorting it and could not. There is nothing to sort. It
is not in pieces. It is one continuous thing, and it does not stop and hand him anything,
and he has never in his life had a continuous thing.

He cannot place his hands. It goes on for some while; he is aware of it as a fault and
aware that the fault is visible; and then Pascual takes him by the wrist and puts the hand
where he wants it, and the relief is out of all proportion, and he understands afterwards
what he has just been given and what it is called and that he asked for one and was refused.

At some point Pascual puts a hand flat against the side of his face and holds it there and
does not move it.

Reggie has seen that done. He has seen it done twice. He gets as far as the second time
and stops.

He does not count. He notices, later, that he has not counted, and cannot establish at what
point he stopped, and there is no duration in his possession at all — not two figures as
there were in August, not one, nothing — and he does not go looking for one, and the not
going looking is a deliberate act and very nearly the only one he performs all night.

Afterwards he lies with his forearm over his face, which is not a thing he does.

"That was worse."

"Yes."

"Considerably worse."

He is asked to go to sleep, and does not, and lies there working at the word, because it
will not do to have a comparative without units.

It is worse in that there was no interval in it. It is worse in that every part of it was
a decision taken without data and there were a great many of them. It is worse in that
there is now no portion of the evening he can be told about in the morning, because there
is no portion of it at which he was absent.

And under that, arriving at about three, in the way these things arrive, unwanted and
entirely finished: what he liked about the other way was that it was done to him. A thing
done to a man is a thing he is not answerable for. He has arranged his whole life on that
principle. He arranged it in a building outside Salisbury for two years, and he arranged
it again on a verandah on the twenty-ninth of August by handing a boy a chart in a tin and
going upstairs to a bottle, and it is the single most consistent finding in the record and
he has just been shown it by a man who was not looking for it.

He is holding Pascual's wrist. He does not remember taking it. He has two fingers on the
artery and has been counting for some time.

He takes the fingers off and keeps hold of the wrist by the flat of it, which is the only
correction available at four in the morning, and goes to sleep.

---

In May he takes the Copenhagen number out of the desk.

He has had it eleven weeks, which is a coincidence and he does not care to look at it.

What he has arrived at, finally, is this: there is no test that clears an act of being a
condition. One cannot prove a negative about one's own motives; the attempt is what he has
been doing since February and it is not converging. But there is a difference between an
experiment and the other thing and it is not in the intention at all, it is in the
apparatus. In an experiment you take the reading. That is the entire distinction and it is
the only one available and it happens to be one he can act on.

So he crosses the yard and puts the journal into Pascual's hand and says something short,
and does not stay, and walks away towards the pens.

He does not look back. He has decided in advance that he will not look back, and it is
sixty yards, and it takes him rather a long time, and there is a face behind him doing
something and he will never know what it did.

He has told a great many people a great many things in his life in order not to be looked
at. This is the first occasion on which he has declined to collect a datum, and it costs
him more than the Tuesday afternoon with Spilsbury, and he does it again on the fourteenth
of June and on the second of August, and it does not get easier in any way he can measure.

---

There is a night in the third week of July on which it helps.

Not much. He notes it because it is the first departure in the series: he comes down in the
morning and the thing is fractionally smaller than it was, and he goes over the night for
a reason and cannot find one, and that is the whole of the entry. Six or seven, he was told.
He has it at eight. He does not mention it, because mentioning it would be reporting a
result to the man who predicted it, and he can imagine the face and would rather not
produce it deliberately.

---

The paper appears in the autumn.

It is well received. Two more animals come in September, and the second co-housing pen is
poured in the same month, and there is talk of four more in the spring, and money, and John
is to have an assistant. Spilsbury writes to say that this is precisely the sort of thing he
had hoped for and that he has said so to the people who need to hear it.

Reggie stands on the apron on the day the new pen is filled and works out, in about the time
it takes the water to come up two feet, what he has actually produced. He has produced a
method by which these animals can be kept alive in captivity. A method by which they can be
kept alive is a licence to take more of them. The figure over ten years is not difficult and
he does it, and does not check it, because he does not need to check it.

That is the second thing he will not be writing down.

He goes back up the steps and passes Pascual in the yard and asks how Delta is eating, and
is told, and it is a good answer, and he is pleased. Both of these are true at once and he
has stopped attempting to reconcile them.

---

The seventh of November. A year to the day, and nobody has said so, and he is fairly sure
that Pascual has the date too and has decided not to have it out loud.

The tide is out. Gamma is at the far end. Delta comes up under the rail after about ten
minutes, as she does now most nights, and looks at the two of them for some time.

Reggie says, to the pen, in an ordinary voice:

"Springs on the twenty-ninth of August, two days past the full. There is a westerly set
through the passage of something over a knot at that state and it holds for the first
seventy miles. Four hundred miles to the Serpent's Mouth. An animal in condition does
eighty in a day if he is not carrying anything and something under fifty if he is. Ten days
at the outside. There is no figure for a man and I have not attempted one."

He does not look round.

Pascual does not say anything at all. He does not ask for the course and he does not say
that he has been waiting fifteen months for it, and Reggie is aware that both of those are
now true, and that there are two men on this station who can be put in a room with a paper,
and that he has arranged it himself, sober, on a Tuesday, having thought about it since
March.

He looks for the alarm. It is not in the place he keeps it. He has no doubt whatever that
it is somewhere and that he will find it at three in the morning, and he stands at the rail
and waits for it and it does not come.

After a while a hand arrives flat between his shoulder blades and stays.

He is standing at the tenth bolt.

He has no note of when he came off the ninth.
