# Residual Effect

The bolts in the pen rail are set at sixteen inches on centre. He has known that since
the rail went in, in the way he knows the cycle of the generator and the fall of the
apron and the number of stairs to the laboratory, which is to say that he did not learn
it and cannot unlearn it and it is simply there when wanted.

He stands at the ninth bolt from the ladder. Pascual stands at the tenth.

Sixteen inches, then. He has it exactly, every night, and he has never once been able to
put it down.

---

The difficulty is that he cannot move.

He worked this out on the second night and it has held since, and it is the sort of thing
that is entirely sound and entirely useless. A man who has spent ten weeks running
conditions on another man is not a man who can afterwards say what any move of his is.
Everything he does now may be a condition.

So: if he moves back, that may be a condition and he may be running it. If he moves in, the
same. And the ninth bolt is where he was standing on the
night of the counting, before he knew he was doing anything at all, and it is therefore the
only position on the whole rail he did not choose, and a position he did not choose cannot
be a condition, and he holds it the way he used to hold the line on a bearing.

Pascual thinks he is being scrupulous. He is fairly sure of that. He would like, once, to
put it correctly — that there is nothing scrupulous about it, that a man frozen in a
doorway is not being polite — and there is no way to say it that would not itself be a
move, and a move may be a condition.

---

He has the nine words. He could produce them at any hour and in his sleep; he has taken
them out perhaps four hundred times and they have not got any easier.

The trouble is not the words. The trigger gives him no difficulty whatever: there is a
sheet in the machine and it has a date on it. It is the other sentence.

*You will not come up here and run conditions on me.*

He cannot establish that going up is not one. To settle it he must examine his own motive,
and the instrument for examining it is the instrument that was faulty in December, and
there is not a second one he can query.

He put it to himself in the third week in its general form and did not care for the
result. One cannot certify one's own motive; the certificate would have to be issued by the
party under examination. There is no procedure that gets round this, and Pascual knew there
was not, and said so, and told him he would have to manage.

---

He manages by asking after Gamma.

He does it every day, in the morning, in the yard, in front of whoever is there. He was
told in February that he had stopped, and it was true — he had, some time in December,
and he cannot now recover the day — and so he asks, and before he asks he has to settle
whether he is asking because he wishes to know, which is permitted, or because he was
told he had stopped, which is a variable being corrected in front of the man it was run
on.

He has never settled it. Not once, in six weeks. He asks anyway, on the ground that the
condition of a receiving animal in a co-housing trial is a legitimate object of daily
enquiry irrespective of the state of the enquirer, which is a piece of reasoning he would
not accept from a second-year student.

It is the same with everything. He carries the crate up from the jetty because it is there
and he is going up anyway; he then spends the afternoon establishing that he did
not carry it in order to be seen carrying it. He does not mention the Copenhagen
number, which came in on the second of March and which he has in his desk, because he
cannot get it out of his desk without knowing what it is for. Civility has become
expensive in a way he could not have described in October, when it cost nothing.

Twice he catches himself designing a way to be pleasant that could not be mistaken for a
condition, and stops, on the grounds that a design is what he is trying to avoid, and
that a man designing his way out of designing has not understood the objection.

---

The exception is Jean.

One night in March, at the rail, Pascual says into the dark that he was hard on that
boy then, and has always been hard on Jean. He does not say anything else about it and
does not appear to want an answer, and Reggie does not give him one; and the sentence is
still there four days later, which is not a thing sentences ordinarily do.

So he begins on Jean. He asks her what she thinks before he says what he thinks. He puts her
initials against the tide work. He says thank you, in a voice he has had to build.

He is bad at it and it is laborious and it is the only thing in six weeks that is any relief
whatever — and the relief is not in the doing of it. It is that it cannot be a manoeuvre.
Pascual is not in it. It is the one piece of conduct left to Reggie about which he does not
have to work out what Pascual is reading, or what he himself is doing it for, because Pascual
very probably does not even know about it.

---

On the ninth of March he takes four.

It works. That is the finding. Three was insufficient in August and the dose was climbing
in December and four, in March, does the whole of it: an hour of nothing usable, then
sleep, then a morning that costs him about as much as one would expect.

He writes down *4* on a clean sheet at half past six in the morning, with the room taking
rather longer to settle than it has any business taking and a headache at the back of his
head which will be there until the afternoon, and dates it, and then sits with the sheet in
front of him for some time, because a man who records a dose
is a man who intends to titrate it, and there is no other reason on earth to write the
figure down.

He opens the drawer to file it and finds the other one, under the mortality tables, where
he put it on the thirtieth of August: *3 — insufficient*, undated, in his own hand.

He cannot destroy either of them. Destroying a record is a worse act than keeping one and
he has held that opinion since he was twenty-two and finds, to his considerable
irritation, that he still holds it. He cannot file them, because there is no heading. So
he writes the date on the August sheet, which is the only operation available to him, and
puts them both back under the tables, and spends rather longer than usual on his collar,
and arrives at breakfast at the time he arrives at breakfast, and asks Pascual how Gamma is
eating.

---

The nights are the arithmetic.

He does not commission it. He walks down to the ladder at half past one and by the time
he is at the rail it has been running for some minutes without being asked, and it is
always the same material: the tide on the twenty-ninth of August, the westerly set
through the passage, four hundred miles, a figure for an animal that can do a fifth of it
in a day and no figure at all for a man.

He has been adding inputs he has no right to. A fisherman at the point said something in
January about the rain in the south and he took it and used it. He knows precisely what
that is. It is not an observation, it is a man feeding his own machine until it produces
a different answer, and the machine does not produce different answers, it produces the
same one, and getting the same one is not reassurance; it is only the same one.

He is not sleeping. He is eating, because Pascual looks at him at mealtimes and he is
not prepared to be looked at and found wanting twice; and nobody has ever watched a man
sleep, so that is where it comes out, and by the end of March he is running on about three
hours and the machinery has never been better. It is remarkable, in fact. It has not been
this good since Salisbury. He has a suspicion about that — that the two things are one system and have always been one
system — which he sets down once, in his head, in a single sentence, and does not develop.

A machine running like that produces more. More of everything, all day, unasked for. He had
expected as much. What he had not expected is the subject.

---

He has a great deal of material on Pascual.

He did not take any of it. That is the part that will not go anywhere when it occurs to him,
at the ninth bolt, one night, with nothing whatever having prompted it: that he has never once
sat down to collect a single item of it, and that there is more of it in his possession than
he holds on any other living person. The man goes up at about eleven unless there is a slide he has not finished,
in which case it is nearer one and he takes the stairs differently. He reads with his glasses pushed into his hair and puts them
on to write. When something has been proposed to him that he intends to refuse he puts both
hands flat on whatever is in front of him before he says anything. He says *ay* at four
distinct pitches and Reggie could sort a hundred of them blind.

None of it was collected. It is simply there when wanted, in the way the bolts are, and he
did not learn it and cannot unlearn it, and there is not a heading in the world it will go
under.

At the rail he can say where the man's hands are without looking. He tested that twice in
the
first week and stopped testing it, on the grounds that testing it did him no good at all.

And on an afternoon at the end of March, crossing the yard with the tide sheets, he gets one
of the sounds.

Not a memory of it. The thing entire and in the right register, at half past two in the
afternoon in full sun, with John forty feet off doing something to a drawing board. He has
had it at intervals since September and has never once been able to call it up on purpose.
He stops walking. He stands in the middle of the yard for what is probably four seconds and
is certainly not fewer, holding a folder of tide sheets, and then goes on and puts them where
they go.

He notes the duration. It is the only operation available to him.

There is one more and it is the one he can do least about.

At some point in the last quarter of the hour, at the rail, Pascual turns towards him. Not far — two or
three inches at the shoulder, no more — and it is arrested before it has finished, every time,
and it happens on every night of the six weeks. Reggie has it at between the fiftieth and the
fifty-fifth minute with a spread of about ninety seconds. He is not looking when it happens.
He does not have to be.

He has never moved. There is no move available to him, and the reason is the spread: he has
the thing to ninety seconds, which means he took a reading, and acting on a reading may be a
condition. To go towards it is to act on it. To go away from it is to act on it.
There is no third move. So he stands at the ninth bolt with his hands where they are and lets
it start and lets it stop, and it happens again the following night, and the night after
that, and he does not move on any of them.

---

The twelfth of April.

Spilsbury's letter came on the Friday. The paper is first-class and there are two
questions about the eleventh of November, and they are good questions, and they are the
questions of a man who intends to go on asking. He has carried the letter about for four
days.

He answers them at the typewriter that evening between eight and twenty past ten. He
answers them honestly. Every figure is correct and every sentence is true and the whole
of it is constructed to route round the middle, which is a female specimen coming off the
bottom after four months and crossing twenty feet of water to a male she had never seen
and putting her hand flat on his face and leaving it there, and he does it well, and at
twenty past ten it is done.

And then the machinery is free.

He has not had it free since November. There has been the paper to route round, and the
routing is heavy work, and it has been taking most of it; and it is done, and
there is nothing in front of the machine at all, and it does not sit idle for so much as a
minute.

It gives him six.

Six other things he could have done on the twenty-ninth of August. Fully worked, in
order, with the objections to each and the objections answered: two of them involving the
Colonel, one involving Mrs Bevan and a form of words about the Navy's own animal, one
that would have required nothing but a telegram and forty-eight hours, and one that is so
simple he has to stand up and walk to the window. He can fault none of them. He goes at
them for perhaps a quarter of an hour, hard, the way he would go at a student's design,
looking for the flaw that makes them impossible, and there is no flaw; there is only that
he did not see them on the night.

He did not see them on the night because on the night he had taken three whiskies in
order to be unable to work out where they had gone.

He sits down on the edge of the desk.

It is not that the dose cost him the course. The course came back, five days later, whole,
and he has it. It is that the dose was taken at nine o'clock and the boy did not go down
until after midnight, and there were three hours in that room in which the man who could
have produced those six things was not there, and Reggie put him out himself, deliberately,
with a measured quantity, because there was one thing he did not want to know and he was
willing to lose the rest of the apparatus to be rid of it.

He goes upstairs at about half past ten.

The question arrives on the stairs — whether this is a condition, whether he is running it —
and he does not take it up. He is aware of not taking it up. He is aware of it the way a man
is aware of a missed step, afterwards, with the weight already down.

He cannot establish it tonight. He is not going to be able to establish it tonight. He goes
up on that, and his hand is not steady on the rail, and he notes the hand and goes on.

---

He gets half of it out.

"The typewriter has been going," he says, "and I —"

And it will not come, and he stands in the corridor with his hands at his sides finding
out why, which is not what he expected to be doing.

It will not come because he has been standing here treating it as a claim. *I cannot stop*
is a proposition, and a proposition has a truth-value, and he cannot support this one: he
stopped on the twenty-ninth of August, at nine in the evening, at a cost of three glasses and
six designs and a man he has no figure for. The stopping is not the difficulty. The stopping
is what he has come about. And he cannot state to this man a thing he cannot support — he has
established that too, this month, at some length, the way one establishes anything, by trying
it and finding the mechanism will not turn — and so there is nothing at the end of the
sentence and he stands there with his jaw locked and produces nothing whatever.

Pascual stands back from the door.

"Seven of nine," he says. "Come in."

He has counted them. He has stood in a doorway at that hour with a man failing in
front of him and he has *counted the words*, and produced the count, and the count is a
number.

*Come in* would not have worked by itself. The number does it.

It is also, he works out on the stairs, a piece of information. Seven of nine leaves two,
and the two are *cannot stop*, and Pascual will have had that in the doorway before Reggie
had it in the corridor, and did not ask for them, and said a number instead.

---

The hour before is not pleasant and he had not expected it to be. He gives an account, out
loud, of what is in the paper and what is not in it, and of what he will be thanked for,
and of what he will therefore be saying four times a year for the rest of his working life;
and the whole time he is doing it he is waiting for the reduction, because that is the
thing one is given to understand about saying a thing aloud, and there is no reduction. It
is precisely the same size at the end as it was at the beginning. He says so, and is told
that it does not, and asks what it is for, then, and is not given an answer he can use.

Then the other thing, and the other thing is different, and he is not ready for it.

He shuts his eyes at the start, out of habit, and opens them again, and after that he
cannot get them shut.

It is the rail. That is what it is; he has the whole of it in about a second and a half.
Shutting his eyes now would be a movement, and he has been standing at the ninth bolt for
six weeks because he cannot certify a movement, and
the paralysis has come up the stairs with him and got into a room where it has no business
whatever. So his eyes stay open. Not from courage. From the same fault that has cost him
six weeks.

And so he is there for all of it.

What he chiefly finds is that he is a cause.

He has been an instrument since he was a child. An instrument is not in its own results;
that is the point of it; a man taking a reading is the one object in the room that does not
appear in the record. And here is a face eight inches from his own doing things, and the
things it does are consequent upon him, and he has no procedure for being the antecedent
of anything, and he can feel himself reaching for the reading and finding that he is on the
wrong side of the glass.

There is no record. That is the second finding and it takes him some time to be sure of it,
because in August there was a record and it was enormous and arrived faster than it could
be laid down and he spent the night sorting it and could not. There is nothing to sort. It
is not in pieces. There is breathing that alters and he cannot say into what; there is
weight on him doing the same thing the breathing is doing, and going faster, and he has no
interval for any of it; there is a hand at the back of his neck that has been there for some
while and he could not give the moment it arrived. None of it presents. It is one continuous
thing, and it does not stop and hand him anything, and he has never in his life had a
continuous thing.

He cannot place his hands. It goes on for some while; he is aware of it as a fault and
aware that the fault is visible; and then Pascual takes him by the wrist and puts the hand
where he wants it.

The relief is out of all proportion to the size of the thing, and he has why before the breath
is out of him, because it is not difficult. He was given an instruction.
Small, exact, and capable of being carried out inside a second. He asked for one of those in
February and was told there would not be one, and has gone six weeks without, and was handed
one in the dark by a man who had not been asked and who almost certainly did not know he was
handing it over.

At some point Pascual puts a hand flat against the side of his face and holds it there and
does not move it. He has had that once before, in August, on the floor, except that in August
it was both.

And Reggie has seen that done. Once, through a rail, in November, by an animal — and he was
holding a stopwatch at the time and did not start it.

He gets that far and stops.

He does not count. He notices, later, that he has not counted, and cannot establish at what
point he stopped, and there is no duration in his possession at all — not two figures as
there were in August, not one, nothing — and he does not go looking for one, and the not
going looking is a deliberate act and very nearly the only one he performs all night.

Afterwards he lies with his forearm over his face, which is not a thing he does.

He is asked to go to sleep, and does not.

He would like to give a verdict and finds that he is not entitled to one, and the reason is
procedural, and knowing it is procedural does not help. The measure has three parts. There
is the hour, and there is the sleeping, and there is the morning; and he has one part of the
three, and a man who returns a result off a third of a measure is not doing anything he
would consent to call work.

The hour was clean. He can say that much and nothing beyond it. There was nothing in it and
nothing came of it and he had no duration at the end of it, and taken by itself it is the
best of the eleven.

The rest of it begins to arrive at about three.

Not the hour. The hour is not there and will not be. What arrives is everything round the
hour: an account of his own conduct across the whole of the evening, entire, in sequence,
every decision with the interval before it and the state of his hands and the precise degree
to which each one was visible from eight inches. It is more than an ordinary evening
produces. It is a good deal more, and all of it is his, and there is nothing to be done with
any of it.

And under that, arriving at the same hour, in the way these things arrive, unwanted and
entirely finished: what he liked about the other way was that it was done to him. A thing
done to a man is a thing he is not answerable for. He has arranged his whole life on that
principle. He arranged it in a building outside Salisbury for two years, and he arranged
it again on a verandah on the twenty-ninth of August by handing a boy a chart in a tin and
going upstairs to a bottle, and it is the single most consistent finding in the record and
he has just been shown it by a man who was not looking for it.

He is holding Pascual's wrist. He does not remember taking it. He has two fingers on the
artery and has been counting for some time.

He takes the fingers off and keeps hold of the wrist by the flat of it, which is the only
correction available at four in the morning, and goes to sleep.

---

In the morning he has the third part of the measure. It is bad. He brings it down to
breakfast, because there is no sense in carrying a figure about.

"That was worse," he says.

Pascual sets the pot down. "Yes."

"Considerably worse. The hour was the best of the eleven. The rest of it cost me about four
hours and I haven't slept."

"Stop that."

"Stop what."

"Ranking them." Pascual sits down opposite him and looks at him for a while without any hurry
at all. "You'll do it anyway and I can't help that, but you're not going to do it out loud
at my table, because the day you tell me one of them came ninth I'll have to sit here and hear
it." He reaches for the pot. "You were working."

"I was doing nothing whatever. That was rather the difficulty."

"You were working at doing nothing whatever, which is harder, and you did it for an hour,
and you did it with your eyes open because you wouldn't let yourself shut them." He pours.
"It cost what work costs. It'll go on costing that for as long as it's work."

Reggie says, "And when does it stop being work."

"When you stop doing it as work."

"That is not a procedure."

"No," Pascual says, and pushes the cup across. "It's a prognosis. You can have it. You can't
act on it — and if you act on it you'll have spoiled it, and I'm perfectly aware of what I've
just put in your hands."

He files it. There is nothing else available to be done with it. He files it under the same
heading as the nine words, which is the heading for instructions with no procedure in them,
and which now has two items in it.

Then he asks how Gamma is eating, and cannot settle, this morning no more than any other,
whether he is asking because he wishes to know.

---

In May he takes the Copenhagen number out of the desk.

He has had it ten weeks, which is a coincidence and he does not care to look at it.

What he has arrived at, finally, is this: there is no test that clears an act of being a
condition. One cannot prove a negative about one's own motives; the attempt is what he has
been doing since February and it is not converging. But there is a difference between an
experiment and the other thing and it is not in the intention at all, it is in the
apparatus. In an experiment you take the reading. That is the entire distinction and it is
the only one available and it happens to be one he can act on.

So he crosses the yard and puts the journal into Pascual's hand and says something short,
and does not stay, and walks away towards the pens.

He does not look back. He has decided in advance that he will not look back, and it is
sixty yards, and it takes him rather a long time, and there is a face behind him doing
something and he will never know what it did.

He has told a great many people a great many things in his life in order not to be looked
at. This is the first occasion on which he has declined to collect a datum. It costs him. He
does it again on the fourteenth of June and on the second of August,
and it does not get easier in any way he can measure.

---

There is a night in the third week of July on which it helps.

Not much. He notes it because it is the first departure in the series: he comes down in the
morning and the thing is fractionally smaller than it was, and he goes over the night for
a reason and cannot find one, and that is the whole of the entry. He does not mention it,
because mentioning it would be reporting a result to the man who predicted it, and he can
imagine the face and would rather not produce it deliberately.

---

The paper appears in the autumn.

It is well received. Two more animals come in September, and the second co-housing pen is
poured in the same month, and there is talk of four more in the spring, and money, and John
is to have an assistant. Spilsbury writes to say that this is precisely the sort of thing he
had hoped for and that he has said so to the people who need to hear it.

Reggie stands on the apron on the day the new pen is filled and works out, in about the time
it takes the water to come up two feet, what he has actually produced. He has produced a
method by which these animals can be kept alive in captivity. A method by which they can be
kept alive is a licence to take more of them. The figure over ten years is not difficult and
he does it, and does not check it, because he does not need to check it.

That is the second thing he will not be writing down.

He goes back up the steps and passes Pascual in the yard and asks how Gamma is eating, and
is told, and it is a good answer, and he is pleased. Both of these are true at once and he
has stopped attempting to reconcile them.

---

The seventh of November. A year to the day, and nobody has said so, and he is fairly sure
that Pascual has the date too and has decided not to have it out loud.

The tide is out. Delta is at the far end. Gamma comes up under the rail after about ten
minutes, as she does now most nights, and looks at the two of them for some time.

Reggie says, to the pen, in an ordinary voice:

"Springs on the twenty-ninth of August, two days past the full. There is a westerly set
through the passage of something over a knot at that state and it holds for the first
seventy miles. Four hundred miles to the Serpent's Mouth. Past the Mouth the water is
brackish a long way in, and there is no harbour on that coast. An animal in condition does
eighty in a day if he is not carrying anything and something under fifty if he is. Ten days
at the outside. There is no figure for a man and I have not attempted one."

He does not look round.

Pascual does not say anything at all. He does not write any of it down. He does not say that
he has been waiting fourteen months for it, and Reggie is aware that he has, and that there
are two men on this station who can be put in a room with a paper,
and that he has arranged it himself, sober, on a Tuesday, having thought about it since
March.

He looks for the alarm. It is not in the place he keeps it. He has no doubt whatever that
it is somewhere and that he will find it at three in the morning, and he stands at the rail
and waits for it and it does not come.

After a while a hand arrives flat between his shoulder blades and stays.

He is standing at the tenth bolt.

He has no note of when he came off the ninth.
