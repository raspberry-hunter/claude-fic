# At Sight

It's the breathing that wakes him.

Pascual lies still and finds out where he is before he moves. It's a habit from barns and decks. It's light, just. There's camphor, and lamp oil, and paper, and the green soap Reggie gets from Kingston. He's in Reggie's bed, on his left side, facing the wall. His right shoulder's loose and warm and nearly right.

And Reggie's along the whole of his back.

There's an arm over his waist, and a hand spread flat on his chest. There's a knee in the crook of his knee, and a shoe, a whole shoe, hooked over his ankle. And there's Reggie's face in the back of his neck, breathing there, slow and even, fast asleep, exactly where the knot was.

*Ay*, thinks Pascual. *No*.

He got over this man in 1956. He did it properly. He checked, after, twice. It had gone.

He lies in the grey and finds out that it hadn't gone anywhere. It had only been waiting for somebody to put their whole weight on it.

He tries to get out. He lifts the arm off his waist by the wrist, gently, and Reggie makes a small cross sound into his neck and takes it back, tighter. So Pascual does the only other thing there is. He turns over. If he can get onto his back he can get out from under the arm and off the end of the bed, and Reggie'll wake up in his own bed on his own, and none of it'll have happened.

It takes a while. The bed's narrow and Reggie's a dead weight. Pascual gets onto his back by degrees, with his shoulder complaining, and Reggie comes with him. He doesn't wake. He simply rearranges himself, the way water does. By the time Pascual's flat on his back, Reggie's got his head on Pascual's good shoulder and his arm across his chest, and his face is in Pascual's neck from the front now instead of the back.

Pascual lies there and looks at the ceiling.

This is worse. This is a great deal worse.

Reggie stirs. He makes a noise, half a word, and his face moves against Pascual's throat. His mouth's right there, under Pascual's jaw. It's warm and a little open, and it presses there, once, sleepily, the way you'd press your face into a pillow you were fond of.

Pascual stops breathing.

He turns his head. He doesn't decide to; he only wants to see. And Reggie's eyes are open, an inch away. They're unfocused without his glasses, grey, soft with sleep. They look at Pascual from so close that he can't possibly be seeing much.

"Oh," says Reggie. His voice is thick and pleased. "It's you."

And he kisses him.

It's slow and warm and not quite aimed. It lands at the corner of Pascual's mouth and finds the rest of it on its own. It tastes of sleep. It's the least careful thing Pascual's ever had from Reggie Tar in twenty years, and it goes through him from his mouth to his heels.

He kisses him back.

He can't not. He's been lying still since before the light came, with Reggie breathing on him, and something in him simply gives, the way the knot gave. He turns towards it. His hand comes up into the short hair at the back of Reggie's head and holds it. He opens his mouth, and Reggie makes a low sound into it, and the kiss stops being sleepy.

He feels the moment Reggie wakes.

He feels it in the body against his: a stillness, all through, like an animal hearing something. The kiss stops for about a heartbeat. Pascual waits for Reggie to pull away.

Reggie doesn't. Reggie's hand comes up off Pascual's chest and takes hold of his jaw, and turns his face a little, to have it better. And Reggie kisses him again. It's awake this time. It's deliberate and hard and it goes on. Reggie comes up on one elbow over him to do it, and Pascual feels the edge of his teeth, and hears himself make a noise he'd have sworn he didn't have in him.

It goes on for some time.

Pascual loses track of it. The ceiling's up there somewhere, and the grey coming in at the open shutters, and the calf blowing out in the pen, and none of it matters. What matters is Reggie's weight, coming down on him a little at a time, as if he were finding out how much of it Pascual would take. Pascual takes all of it. Reggie's thumb moves along his jaw. Reggie kisses the corner of his mouth, and then his lower lip, slowly, like a man reading a line twice to be sure of it, and then comes back and kisses him properly again. Pascual's hand in his hair has gone from holding to hanging on. Their stubble scrapes, and Reggie makes a small pleased sound about it and does it again on purpose. Somewhere in there Pascual thinks, quite clearly, *twenty years*. Then he can't think anything, because Reggie's knee has come over his, and Reggie's saying his name into his mouth, low, as if he'd only just learned it.

Then it stops.

It stops because Reggie stops. He goes rigid all at once, with his mouth an inch off Pascual's, and his hand still on Pascual's jaw, and his breath coming short. Pascual opens his eyes. Reggie's looking down at him from very close, and he can see now, and his face is going from one thing to another faster than Pascual can follow.

"Oh, *God*," says Reggie.

---

He's sitting up against the wall. He doesn't remember getting there. He's got his knees up and his back to the plaster and his hand over his mouth, as though he could put the thing back.

Pascual's lying on his back in the middle of the bed, with his shirt off and the blanket round his waist, looking at him. His face is doing everything at once.

"I'm sorry," says Reggie. "Pascual. I'm so sorry. I was half asleep. I didn't — it doesn't —"

"It doesn't *what*?"

"Count," says Reggie. "It needn't. I was asleep, and you were — it was a very long day, and nobody would hold either of us to —"

"Half asleep," says Pascual.

"Yes."

Pascual sits up. He does it slowly, with his right arm held in. He's not looking at Reggie now. He's looking at the wall at the foot of the bed.

"Who did you think I was?" he says.

Reggie stares at him.

"What?"

"You said *oh, it's you*." Pascual's voice is very level. It's the level one he uses when an animal's worse than he'd hoped. "Like you'd been expecting somebody. You woke up with a man in your bed, half asleep, and you said *oh, it's you*, and kissed him. So I'm asking. Who'd you think it was?"

It goes into Reggie like a gaff.

He opens his mouth to say *how dare you*, and it's halfway out before he hears the rest. He hears what's under it. There's a drawer in his desk with a letter in it, and a lamp on the table that burned every night for nineteen years, and the whole station knew what it was for. There's a man in Norway who's been dead two years. And there's Pascual, who sat on the floor of Jean's office in May 1971 with his forehead against Reggie's arm, while Reggie held the photograph.

It's a fair question. It's the fairest question anybody's ever put to him, and Pascual's asking it looking at the wall.

"You," says Reggie.

Pascual doesn't turn round.

"I thought it was you," says Reggie. "I knew it was you. You smell of the Colonel's knees. Nobody else on God's earth smells of the Colonel's knees."

Pascual makes a sound. It isn't a laugh. It's a noise like a laugh that's been hit.

"Except the Colonel," says Reggie, in fairness. "And I was reasonably sure it wasn't the Colonel."

This time it is a laugh. It comes out of Pascual before he can stop it, one great bark, and he claps his left hand over it and glares at Reggie over the top.

"Why are you making me *laugh*?" he says, through his fingers. "I'm trying to have a *row* with you."

"I'm answering the question."

"You're *not*, you're —"

"I am. It's exactly the thing. I had my face in your neck and it smelled of wintergreen and horse, and your hair's grey and it's short, and you're twice the size of —" He stops. He goes on. "Of anybody else. I didn't have to wonder. I opened my eyes and there you were, and I was glad. That's what *oh, it's you* means, in English. It means *good*."

Pascual turns his head then, and looks at him.

"Then what's *it doesn't count*?"

"I was trying to —"

"You were trying to give me a way out." Pascual's voice isn't level any more. It's coming up. "You were sat there against that wall with your hand over your mouth, and you'd decided, in about a second, that I'd want one."

"I —"

"That I'd want to get up and go to breakfast and say *what a long day that was, Reggie*, and never —"

"Pascual —"

"It *counts*!" It comes out of him all at once, the way it does. "It counts to *me*! You don't get to say it doesn't!"

Reggie sits against the wall and doesn't say anything.

"Twenty years," says Pascual. He's breathing hard. "I've sat on the end of this bed twenty years. I got over you in 'fifty-six. I *did*."

"Pascual —"

"I was *thorough* about it! And I woke up this morning with your shoe on my ankle, and it turns out I made a bad job of it. And then you *kiss* me, and it's the best thing since the *calf*, and you sit there and tell me it needn't *count* —"

He stops. He puts his left hand over his face.

"'Fifty-six," says Reggie.

"From 'fifty-three. On and off." Behind the hand. "Mostly off. There was a woman at the Bay, and a fellow off the survey ship in 'sixty-two, and they were good. I didn't sit about."

"I didn't think you had."

"But it was you, if you'd once looked round. And you didn't, and that was fair; you'd a lamp lit. I'd no complaint. I had Saturdays."

Reggie sits very still.

"You threw a bucket at me," he says.

"That was about the *calcium*."

"In 'fifty-four."

"It was *also* about the calcium." Pascual takes his hand off his face. His eyes are wet and he doesn't seem to know it. "Don't you make me laugh. I'm shouting at you."

"I know you are."

"I'm not *finished*."

"I know."

"Then *answer* me. Why'd you go on?" Pascual's looking straight at him. "You woke up. I felt you wake up. You stopped for about half a second, and then you took hold of my face and went on. You weren't half asleep then. So don't you sit there and tell me it was the bed."

Reggie looks at him.

And it's there. That's the worst of it. He doesn't have to go looking. He goes to answer, and finds he can't say anything that isn't it.

"No," he says. "It wasn't the bed. I was awake. I knew exactly who it was and exactly what I was doing, and I wanted to go on. So I did." His voice goes flat and hard. "And I didn't ask you."

Pascual blinks.

"That's the thing," says Reggie. "That's the one thing. I swore it on a step in 1957. After Frank. I had my hand on your jaw and I turned your face to suit myself, and I didn't ask. I just took it, because I wanted it. That's what I did in the lift, and Frank paid for it."

"*Reggie* —"

"I know what I'm talking about. I've had sixteen years to learn it. I don't get to have a thing because I want it and let somebody else —"

"*Did I ask you*?"

Reggie stops.

"Did I ask you?" says Pascual again. He's gone red to the ears. "You kissed me asleep, and I kissed you *back*, awake, with my hand in your hair. I'd been awake an hour. I knew exactly who *you* were. Did I ask *you* first?"

"That's not the —"

"So I'm a thief too, am I? The pair of us? Robbing each other in our shirt-sleeves in your bed at six in the morning?"

"Pascual, that isn't what —"

"It's *exactly* what! It's your rule, Reggie, I'm only reading it!" His left hand comes out, flat. "What did you *take*? From me? Tell me what I've lost. I'll wait."

Reggie opens his mouth.

Nothing comes out.

"Nothing," says Pascual. "I've lost nothing. I'm sat here with more than I had last night. Nobody's paid for anything."

"Yet."

Pascual stops.

It comes out of Reggie by itself, short and cold, and he's glad of it, because it's true and somebody has to say it. "Nobody's paid *yet*."

And they both, at the same moment, look at the shutters.

They're wide open. They've been open all night. There's the row out there in the early light, and the path down to the pen, and the corner of the long room. Baptiste does the six o'clock line at the rail, every morning, rain or shine. From the rail of the north pen, if you turned round, you'd see straight into the hut at the end of the row.

Pascual's off the bed before Reggie is. He gets the shutter on the pen side and swears at the catch, one-handed. Reggie gets the one by the door. The light goes to slats.

They stand in the middle of the floor and look at each other.

"She'd not have turned round," says Pascual, low. "She'll have had her eyes on the calf."

"You don't know that."

"No." He rubs his face. "No. I don't."

"It's Jean's licence," says Reggie. He keeps his voice down, and keeps it plain. "Her name's on it. And Halloran's sentence is in a file in Kingston with my name beside it, and you're in it too, in the shape of a window. That's what *yet* means."

"I know what it means. I've known since August 'seventy-one."

"Then you know I'm right."

"I know you're right about the *shutters*." Pascual's voice starts to go up again. He pulls it down, visibly, with a hand over his face. When he takes the hand away he's quieter, and it's worse. "You're right about the shutters. You're wrong about the rest. You think the way to keep me out of that file is to decide it for me. On your own, in thirty seconds, against a wall. *It doesn't count*. And then we'd go to breakfast."

Reggie doesn't say anything.

"You did that in 'fifty-seven," says Pascual. "You decided what I could carry and didn't tell me for fourteen years. And on the step I told you you'd no right, and you said *I know*. I heard you say it."

"I did say it."

"Then *don't*. Not again. Not about this."

He's standing in the slatted light with his arm held in and his face doing all of it.

"You can tell me it doesn't count to *you*. If that's so, tell me, and I'll go to breakfast, and I'll be all right. I was all right before. But you tell me that. Don't you tell me it doesn't count to me."

The hut's very quiet. Out in the north pen there's a small high blow, and after it a big slow one.

Reggie stands there.

He could say it. He could say *it doesn't count to me*. Pascual would go to breakfast. Pascual would be all right, eventually. He'd been all right for seventeen years. The file would stay closed. Jean's licence would stay clean. It's the safe thing, and the kind thing, and it'd be a lie.

He finds he isn't going to say it. He isn't *able* to. It isn't principle. It's simpler than that. He opens his mouth to try the shape of it and his whole self won't let it out.

"It counts," says Reggie.

Pascual doesn't move.

"It counts to me," says Reggie. "I don't know how much. I haven't —" He stops. That's not true either. "No. I do know. I haven't had time to look at it properly, and I don't need to. I had my hand on the back of your neck last night for half an hour after the knots were out. I didn't stop because I didn't want to. I thought it was because you were tired."

"I *was* tired."

"It wasn't that."

"Reggie —"

"And I woke up and it was you and I was *glad*. Before I was awake enough to be anything else, I was glad. You can't get much further down than that. I've looked."

Pascual's face goes through something. It goes through a great deal.

"Is it instead?" he says, very low. "Tell me it's not instead. Of him. Now he's —"

"He's in the drawer," says Reggie. "He's staying there. I'll not take him out and I'll not pretend he isn't in it. But you're not in the drawer, Pascual, you're in my *bed*. You were here before the lamp was lit. You filled it."

"I filled it for *you*."

"I know. I know you did. I'm only just finding out how many things you did for me." His voice goes, and he lets it go. "It's not instead. It can't be. You were here the whole time."

Pascual makes the sound again, the laugh that's been hit. He puts his hand over his mouth.

"You can't say a thing like that to a man before he's had his tea," he says, behind it.

"It's twenty to seven."

"*Reggie*."

"And I've still not asked." Reggie holds on to that. It's the one thing he's got. "I've told you it counts, and I've told you about the neck. But I haven't asked you anything. So I'm —"

"*Reggie*."

"— asking. It'll weigh what I said. The file, and Jean, and my —"

Pascual takes his hand away from his mouth. "You *kissed* me."

"Yes, that's rather the —"

"Awake. You stopped, and you looked at me, and you took hold of my face, and you went *on*." Pascual's up off the bed. "What d'you think that *was*? That was asking. That's what it *is*. You've a doctorate, Reggie, you read *everything*, and you —"

He doesn't finish. He crosses the two steps between them and takes Reggie's face in both hands, the right one a little stiffly, and kisses him.

It's short and hard and furious. It knocks Reggie back half a step against the table, and the lamp rattles. Then Pascual pulls back an inch.

"And that's the answer," says Pascual. "In case you're slow."

Reggie stands with his back against the table and his mouth stinging and can't say anything at all.

"Twenty years, *hombre*." Pascual's voice cracks down the middle. "What did you *think* I'd say?"

"I was going to do it properly."

"You were doing it in one shoe."

Reggie looks down. He's got one shoe on. The other one's under the bed somewhere, where it came off in the night on Pascual's ankle.

"It was the best I could do at short notice," he says.

Pascual's hands are still on his face. Reggie lifts his own, slowly, and puts it over one of them, and then takes it away again and puts it on Pascual's jaw instead, slowly, so he can feel himself do it. The stubble's rough under his palm. The jaw moves as Pascual swallows. He waits a second, with his hand there, to see if Pascual will move away.

Pascual doesn't. Pascual's eyes are wet and furious and he's holding very still under the hand, and he says, "*Well?*"

Reggie laughs, and kisses him.

It's nothing like the one in the bed, or the one just now. The first he woke into halfway through. The second was done to him, and he'd not have missed it for the world. This one he's holding from the start, and he can feel all of it. Pascual's mouth is warm, and it opens slowly, and he breathes out through his nose against Reggie's cheek, long and shaking. His left hand comes up and takes hold of Reggie's shirt at the collar. His right comes up too, carefully, as far as it can, and settles on Reggie's shoulder.

"Your arm," says Reggie against his mouth.

"It's fine."

"Is it?"

"It's *fine*. You fixed it." Pascual's forehead comes down against his. "Don't you *dare* stop over my arm."

"I'm not stopping."

"You'd better not. I've been lying in that bed since before six with your breath on my neck and your shoe on my foot, and I'll —"

"You'll what?"

"I don't know," says Pascual, helplessly. "Something. Kiss me again."

He does. It goes on a while. At some point his glasses would have gone crooked, if he'd had them on. They're on the table by the lamp where he put them last night. He doesn't need them. He doesn't need to see Pascual to know where he is.

When they come apart the second time they're both breathing hard. Pascual's got his hand spread on the back of Reggie's neck, in the exact place Reggie had his last night. He's moving his thumb there, just slightly, in the short hair.

"That's my trick," says Reggie.

"I've had it off you."

"You've had everything useful off me."

"No," says Pascual. "Not yet." His thumb stops. His face, a few inches off, is doing a very particular thing, and Reggie can read it perfectly. "May I?"

Reggie goes still.

"May you what?"

"Don't make me say it at twenty to seven in the morning, Reggie. You know what."

"You're asking."

"I caught it off you. It's catching." His voice drops. "Unless you'd rather go to breakfast."

"I would not rather go to breakfast."

"Then answer."

"Yes," says Reggie. "Yes. Please."

---

The bed's too narrow. They both know it; they slept in it. It turns out it doesn't matter nearly as much as it ought to.

Pascual gets his shirt off him. He does it the way he's done it on the jetty a hundred times, when Reggie's come out of the water with no feeling in his hands, and then he stops doing it that way. His hands slow down. They open the last button and then spread, flat, on Reggie's chest, and stay there.

"Too fast," says Pascual, under his breath.

"You're counting."

"I'm not counting. I'm noticing." He bends his head and puts his mouth where his hands are. "There's a difference."

Reggie makes a sound he doesn't recognise.

The other shoe comes off. Pascual does that too, crouching with a grunt, and throws it over his shoulder into the corner with the first one, and says *that's for my ankle*. Reggie laughs, and it turns into something else, because Pascual's still down there and has put his mouth on the inside of Reggie's knee through his trousers, and then his hands are at the waistband.

"*Pascual*."

"Asked," says Pascual, not looking up. "Answered. You said please."

After that it goes quickly and slowly at once. Reggie gets Pascual onto his back on the bed, carefully, on the left, the side that doesn't complain. Then Reggie's over him, on his knees, with nothing on at all, in the slatted light. Pascual looks up at him.

He looks for a long time.

Reggie lets him. He doesn't know what his own face is doing. He's got no voice on, and no smirk, and nothing ready. He's fifty-four and thin and there's grey on his chest, and he's being looked at by a man who's known him twenty years and is only now seeing this. He finds he doesn't mind. He finds he rather wants to be looked at.

"Well?" says Reggie. It comes out rough.

"You want feeding."

"*Not now*."

"No." Pascual's hand comes up and lies on Reggie's hip, broad and warm. His thumb moves on the bone. "No. Not now. Come here."

Reggie comes down to him. He comes down along the whole of him, chest to chest, and Pascual's arms go round him, both of them, the right one a little stiffly. Reggie can feel how much Pascual wants him, hard against his hip, and he hears himself make a sound at it that's nearly a laugh and isn't.

"*Ay*," says Pascual into his hair. "*Reggie*."

"I know."

"You *don't* know. You found out at six."

"Twenty to seven."

"You're going to be *impossible* about this."

"I'm going to be exactly as impossible as I've been for twenty years," says Reggie. "You'll hardly notice."

Pascual's laugh goes through the whole of both of them. Then his hand goes down Reggie's back, slow, over the small of it and lower, and round, between his thighs. And Reggie stops laughing.

He's slick there already, and aching. He hadn't known how much until the hand found him. His breath goes, all at once. He puts his face down in Pascual's neck, in the wintergreen and the horse, and his hips move into the hand before he's decided anything.

"There?" says Pascual. His voice is low and not steady.

"*Yes*."

"Like that?"

"Slower. No — like that. *Like that*."

"*Así*?"

He's heard that word bellowed across the north pen at students for twenty years, and it's never once sounded like this.

"*Así*," says Reggie into his neck. "Yes. *Así*. Keep on."

Pascual keeps on. He does it the way he did the calf: patiently, with his eyes half shut, all in the hands. He listens with his whole body. He changes when Reggie's breath changes, before Reggie's asked for anything. Reggie hears himself saying Pascual's name into his neck. It's low, and uneven, and it doesn't stop.

"You're shaking," says Pascual.

"I know I'm shaking."

"Good." His other hand moves in Reggie's hair. "Good. Let it."

"Pascual." Reggie pushes himself up, on his hands, over him. He can hardly get the breath for it. "I want — you. Not your hand. *You*."

Pascual looks up at him and his face does the whole thing, every part of it, all at once.

"Then have me," he says. "I'm asking you to."

So Reggie does. He sinks down onto him, slowly, with Pascual's hands on his hips steadying him. They both stop halfway and just breathe, stunned, with their eyes on each other in the half-light. Then Reggie goes the rest of the way, and Pascual's head goes back into the pillow with a sound Reggie will think about for the rest of his life.

"*Oh*," says Reggie. He sounds astonished even to himself.

"All right?"

"Don't ask me that."

"I'm *asking*."

"*Yes*. God. Yes. Give me — a minute."

"Take a minute. Take ten." Pascual's thumbs move on his hips. "You're not going anywhere. You're in your own bed."

It's slow when it starts. Reggie finds the way of it, rocking, and Pascual's hands hold him and move with him. Pascual doesn't hurry him at all, and Reggie loves him for it. He'd known that for about forty minutes and he knows it now to the bone. The bed creaks. Out in the north pen the calf's calf blows, high and thin, and neither of them does anything about it.

"You're looking at me," says Pascual.

"You looked at me."

"I'm allowed. I'm the vet."

"Then I'm the —" He loses it. Pascual's hips have come up to meet him and there's nothing left to say anything with. He puts one hand flat on Pascual's chest to hold himself up, and under it Pascual's heart is going like a pump, and he doesn't count it either.

"*Reggie*."

"Yes."

"Reggie. *Reggie*." It isn't *hombre*. It isn't bellowed across a pen in two languages. It's low and rough and over and over, as if it were a word Pascual had been keeping somewhere safe for twenty years and had only just been let take out and use.

It goes through Reggie like the tide turning. It lifts him off the coral all at once, and he holds on to Pascual with both hands and lets it. He's shaking all over, with his head down and his mouth open, saying something he'll not remember. Pascual holds him through it with both hands on his hips. And then Pascual's breath catches, and goes, and he says something that isn't Spanish, or not any Spanish Reggie's picked up in twenty years at the rail. Whatever it is, Reggie's fairly sure nobody in Pascual's family would've let him say it in the house. Then he pulls Reggie down onto his chest and holds on.

---

Afterwards they lie in the middle of the much-too-narrow bed. Pascual's on his back and Reggie's half on top of him, with his face in Pascual's neck, where it was when he woke up.

Pascual's fingers have found his wrist. Reggie lets them.

"Well?" says Reggie.

"Hundred and two."

"Is that bad?"

"It's *vain*." He sounds indecently pleased. "It'll come down."

"I've got the bottle in my shirt."

"I know where your shirt is. It's on the floor with your shoes." Pascual turns his head. His mouth is against Reggie's hair. "Do you want it?"

Reggie considers the question honestly, which is new.

"No," he says. "I'll tell you if I do."

"You'll tell me *first*."

"First."

"Good." The fingers stay on his wrist. After a moment they start, very lightly, to keep time.

Reggie lies there and feels him do it and doesn't say anything. He doesn't think he could, just at the moment.

"Jean," says Pascual, after a while.

"Yes."

"This morning."

"She'll be in the office at eight. It's Sunday; she does the transects." Reggie lifts his head. "Together."

"Together." Pascual's quiet a moment. "I'll let you talk."

"You won't."

"I'll let you *start*."

"And then you'll tell her about the calcium."

"I'll tell her what's *relevant*," says Pascual, "and it's a quarter past seven, and you've no shoes on, and I'm going to sleep for twenty minutes with you on me, and you're not to move."

"I wasn't going to move."

"You woke up and *took your arm off me*, in 'fifty-six."

"I did not."

"You did something like it," says Pascual, and shuts his eyes. "Twenty minutes."

Reggie puts his face back in Pascual's neck.

He doesn't sleep. He lies there and listens. There's the calf and her calf, blowing out in the pen. There's Baptiste's pen scratching the quarter-hour at the rail, faint through the shut slats, or he fancies there is. And there's Pascual's breathing going slow underneath him, and Pascual's two fingers going slack on his wrist and staying there.

He doesn't count anything. He lies still, and is where he is.

---

Jean looks up from the transects at five past eight.

She looks at the two of them standing in her doorway. She looks at Reggie, who has his glasses on and his hair wet and a face so carefully arranged that she'd have known something was wrong if she'd never met him. She looks at Pascual, who has nothing arranged at all, and never has, and is red to the ears.

"Shut the door," says Jean.

Pascual shuts it.

"Well?" says Jean.

Reggie opens his mouth. He's got it all in order. He's had since half past seven to put it in order, lying in the bed with Pascual asleep on him, and he'd got it down to three minutes.

"We'd like to ask you something," he says, "about the licence —"

"Oh, for heaven's *sake*," says Jean. "Yes."

Reggie stops.

"I haven't asked you."

"You've got Pascual's shirt on," says Jean. "Inside out, with the sleeves rolled four times. And Pascual's in his vest, carrying yours."

Reggie looks down. Pascual looks down at the shirt over his arm.

"It wouldn't go over my shoulders," says Pascual, to nobody.

"I've got eyes, Reggie. I'm a field biologist." She puts her pen down. "Yes. Be careful. Shutters. Not on the verandah, not in the boat, not in front of the students, and not in front of Hester, who'll be kind about it and tell everyone at the Bay how kind she's being. The Colonel's eighty-three and I'll tell him myself, when I've thought how."

"Jean —"

"And *not* in front of Baptiste. She's twenty-three. She's got the book to think of."

Pascual makes a strangled noise.

"What?" says Jean.

"Nothing," says Pascual. "Nothing. The shutters were — nothing."

Jean looks at him for a long moment, and then at Reggie, and then at the ceiling.

"Go and change your shirts," she says. "Both of you. And then go away."

They go.

Out on the verandah, Baptiste is at the rail with the green book open, writing the eight o'clock line. She looks up as they come out of the office. She takes in the two of them, and the shirts, and whatever their faces are doing.

"Good morning, Dr Tar. Good morning, Dr Pobil," she says. "Resp. twelve and forty. Both feeding. Quiet night."

Pascual stops dead.

"Quiet," he says.

"Very quiet," says Baptiste. She looks back down at the book. "I didn't turn round once."

She writes the line. Reggie reads it upside down, because he reads everything upside down, and has since 1946. *08.00. 12/40. Both feeding. Remarks: —*

There's nothing after the dash at all.

Pascual's reading it too, at his elbow, slowly, with his lips moving. He gets to the dash and stops there. His ears, which had been coming back to their proper colour on the stairs, go again.

"She's to have a rise," he says, very low, as they go down the steps.

"She's a Student. They don't get rises."

"Then she's to have the Colonel's half-crown."

"The Colonel didn't win."

"Then I'll *buy* it off him," says Pascual, "and give it her," and goes down the path to the huts in his vest, with Reggie's shirt over his arm, not three feet away.
