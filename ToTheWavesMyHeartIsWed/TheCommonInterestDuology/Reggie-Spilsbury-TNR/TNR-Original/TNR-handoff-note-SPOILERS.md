# The Next Room — handoff note

**SPOILERS: this covers all ten drafted installments.**

The arc is an AU branching from 15-TCI *Number 197*: in November 1946 nobody comes up the stairs, Spilsbury spends his one preference at thirty-four instead of forty-four, and he and Reggie are together for the next ten years.

## The spine

- Spilsbury gets better, but only in the Reggie direction. He counts Reggie's costs and nobody else's. That makes him a partner who will never be a brake, which is what the arc is about.
- Reggie's flaw runs with a co-signer. Canon's secret unhappiness is gone, so the work goes further and the exit has to come from self-diagnosis instead.
- Nobody lies. Neither man has ever told the other an untruth, and the whole arc turns on that being true.
- Running count: Spilsbury declines his own arithmetic three times, always for Reggie — the passage in 1946, Nicholson's money and job in 1950, the report in 1954. He keeps the tally in his notebook.
- "We made each other" (10) is the thesis line.

## Continuity invented in this arc

- **The Labs:** a Ministry of Supply establishment near London, canal behind it, long tank on the ground floor split into glass-partitioned bays. Reggie there 1943–29 November 1950; adjoining rooms with Spilsbury from 1946; the connecting door open from 15 November 1946 until Hollis shuts it for the draught.
- **The ledger:** forty-one animals received since April 1943, seven alive in October 1949.
- **Nineteen:** male, just over nine feet, old healed forearm fracture, white scar on the shoulder. Habituated to two taps on the rail; offered his forearm on the coping stone, and again on the board.
- **The partitions:** blacked out with bitumen in 1947 at Reggie's suggestion, after Four and Five held hands either side of a four-inch window for 119 minutes. Variance down forty per cent. This is the AU's version of the bond mechanism being destroyed by the people who could have found it.
- **The coat:** built at thirteen in a school corridor; Reggie works out on a half-landing in 1949 that it doesn't only deflect people, it makes them give him things, and that he currently spends it on permission to continue.
- **Spilsbury:** Alexander; Cambridge; a first-floor flat off the Fulham Road; a bank deposit box since 1939; nine notebooks; a jar of shillings; likes to be held down and asked for it once, in November 1950. Sacked from Dewar & Lecky for putting three opinions in writing.
- **Nicholson:** Frank, wife Margaret, a house in Lee, watercolours. The Southport optical firm and the quarterly money are Spilsbury's doing, at one remove.
- **Reggie's exile:** the Bureau at Oxford, 1950–52, £200 a year, fifty-eight miles; sails for the Martin 28 August 1952. Letters by the estate boat every third Tuesday, written as technical correspondence about fixatives, signed with two initials.
- **Pascual:** thirty-nine on arrival in November 1952, sister in Toulouse, Lisbon aquarium and Florida before this. Knows there is a man by February 1953; Spilsbury concludes in 1954 that he knows it is him. Four years, no questions.
- **The 1955 paper:** Pobil first, Leslie second, Tar third. The 1951 monograph is retracted in December 1955 because only four of 211 animals were ever paired.

## Canon anchors kept

Nicholson's arrest and ruin; Reggie leaving in autumn 1950 because he saw he would otherwise keep doing the work; Alpha dead in a box in 1952; the eleven-day separation, the verandah row and Beta over the lip on the eleventh day, found by Charles; Jean's soundings; Beta and Charles into the water on 19 May 1954; the eighteen minutes with the file; the engagement concluded 9 February 1956, 197 taken at four in the morning on the eleventh, fifty-one days, nineteen on live fish, thirty-one on the bottom, dead 6 May, nothing on the post-mortem.

## Open threads

1. **11 — the long table at eight, Pascual POV.** He currently likes Spilsbury on four years of evidence that the man kept Reggie company. Twenty minutes of the account will give him the rest of the evidence. He is also the only person in the room who understands what the record is worth, because the mechanism is his own 1954 observation of Gamma and the calf, confirmed at a price he'd never have agreed to pay.
2. **Whether they ever break up.** C expected it over Beta and Charles and it didn't happen, because Spilsbury gives Reggie whatever he asks for. The limit got measured in February 1956 instead: Reggie asked him to resign Dewar & Lecky and he said no, and lost the post four days later anyway. That is the closest thing to a fracture the arc has produced.
3. **TCI's machinery** — the ledger with no column for *why*, live animals through colonial ports, the hearing — is seeded at the end of 10 and not yet written.
4. **C holds that Beta lived.** Spilsbury argues in 07 that he was dead before the boy was. Both readings are live; nothing in the arc settles it.

## Working conventions used

Reggie and Pascual's calibration sheets in the project govern dialogue: contraction rates (Reggie at or above 4 per 100 words of dialogue, Pascual always above Reggie), at most two speaking turns of 50+ words, at least three short volleys, "eleven" only where it is the clinical record, at most one prolepsis, and neither man saying "I always" or "I never" about himself. C asked for the metrics to be reported with each file up to 07 and to be skipped from 08 on.
