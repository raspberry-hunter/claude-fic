# Pascual Pobil — Calibration Sheet

## 1. The disposition

**He always tries to be warm, and he has a temper that gets out.** Warmth is the
default and the intention; the temper is what happens when the default fails. These are
not two equal modes and he is not a man of moods.

**Its most reliable trigger is people behaving in ways that endanger animals, or
people** — in that order. Not the only trigger; other things get to him. But it is not
general irritability and it does not fire at incompetence, slowness, or being
disagreed with.

**A sick animal or an injured person gets only the warmth.** The heat is
for the person who caused it, never for the casualty.

**He loves the work, and he likes animals and people, and cares about them a great
deal.** He is entirely practical about all of it and would not put any of it that way.

---

## 2. What he does

**He explodes when he is upset.** *"You don't know that!" Pascual explodes at him. "He
could be in kidney failure and you wouldn't* know. *He wouldn't know either."* Note
what this is about: an animal was on concrete for hours and nobody fetched him.

**He starts hot and pulls himself back, visibly.** *"In your professional medical
opinion, boy!" Pascual starts heatedly, and then visibly calms himself.* Elsewhere:
"quite clearly only holding on to his composure by a thread"; "he rubs a hand over his
face."

**He modulates to who is in front of him.** On the morning after the worst of it, with
Charles distraught, he is "rather more matter-of-fact than he was the day before,
almost fatalistic" — *"Can't be helped. Muscle damage is the devil."* The heat had
gone the moment the casualty was the one in the room, also once he has had some time to compose himself and knows what he is dealing with.

**He grumbles, grimaces, and speaks gruffly and drily.** The dryness goes to peers —
*"Ah, Reggie, Jean. Showing up just when the work is over."* The gruffness goes to
juniors and carries no malice.

**He is openly delighted by work and says so.** *"Such a pleasure to be working with an
animal who isn't declining."* He "appears to be enjoying himself a great deal" during a
physical everyone else finds unpleasant. He is pleased by a wound that healed well:
*"Hm. I'm surprised that healed as well as it did. Very good."*

**He gives credit away, unprompted and in public.** Asked for a hypothesis: *"I expect
it was Charles,"* in front of Jean and John, then *"No offence, Jean, John, but you two
were hardly getting that behaviour out of him."* He confirms Charles's data against his
own — *"Agrees with his food logs"* — and nods approvingly.

**He looks after people in practical terms and does not discuss it.** Food, sleep,
glasses. *"Go eat. You're no use to him if you keel over from hunger."* *"I'll stay
here until you get back, all right?"* *"Where are your glasses, boy? You'd better get
them. I won't have a helper who can't see."* He makes Charles nap, and delegates the
same job to Jean when he can't do it himself.

**He jokes deadpan and is not always recognised.** To the mermaid: *"But no climbing
out of the pen,"* said sternly, "and it takes a moment for Charles to work out that
he's joking, in his gruff way."

**He does not back down when he is right.** He holds Reggie's eyes across the rail
until Reggie drops his own — *"You have your results, Reggie. You don't need more."*
**He wins because he is right and Reggie knows it.** The not-looking-away is a
consequence of being right, not the method. He backs down readily when he is not right.

---

## 3. How he talks

**He contracts heavily.** 5.9 per 100 words of dialogue, the highest rate of any
principal in the book. He is the least formal speaker on the station.

> "I'm not stupid."
> "If you'd woken me we could have got several men to help and he wouldn't have struggled so much."
> "I can't sedate him, it'd kill him, and after that stunt, he is going to need to rest."
> "You're no use to him if you keel over from hunger."
> "Can't be helped."

**He usually does not deliver prepared statements.** He recounts, grumbles, interrupts himself,
and asks four questions at once when alarmed: *"What's going on, Charles? Why do I have
a specimen here who was out of his pen last night? What happened last night? And why
are you here? Are you all right?"*

**He calls Charles "boy."** Constantly, in every register — reprimand, instruction,
affection. He insists on his own first name in return: *"oh, come on, boy, you're the
only one who still calls me that; call me Pascual."*

---

## 4. What he withholds

Very little. Almost everything he feels is audible within a sentence of feeling it.

**He keeps the row out of the wrong room.** He gets Reggie to yield in front of Jean
and Charles, states the medical position, and then asks for the private word —
*"Reggie. I'd like to have a word with you —"* He is going to let loose, and he is
going to do it in the corridor. Having already shouted at him in front of the whole
station the night before, he is being marginally more discreet about it this time.

**He does not explain his care.** Naps, meals, glasses are issued as instructions with
medical justifications attached. He would explain if asked, or if he judged it necessary.

## 5. Facts

- Dr Pobil. Anatomist, "doubling and tripling as vet and pathologist."
- Came to the Institute not long before Charles; there before him.
- Does the post-mortems. Would have done Alpha's had he been there.
- Wants more to work with: *"No — not until that wretched Tar gives me more to work
  with."* This is a man short of material, not a man refusing a job.
- Treats Tar as an equal and rows with him at a volume the whole station can place.

---

## 6. Documented drift, and its mechanism

**Measured.** His contraction rate falls from **5.9** in *Waves* to **1.5** and then
**0.0** in later material. In the piece where it reaches zero, every marker of
volatility disappears too — no explosion, no grumble, no visible recovery from heat. He
becomes a controlled instrument delivering a prepared statement he has composed in
advance.

**The mechanism is a vacancy, not proximity.** That piece is the one from which Tar is
absent. The scene still needed someone in the position of *the man delivering a
difficult itemised thing under strain*, and with the usual occupant off-page, Pascual
inherited the register — including the stage-direction shape ("He almost smiled and did
not quite").

**The rule:** when a character who carries a distinctive register is absent from a
scene, check whether the register has moved rather than gone. Registers do not vacate;
they get picked up.

## 7. Author rulings (not in the text)

*Waves* gives him no physical description at all.

- **Build.** On the bigger side, just shy of six feet, strongly built and strong in
  general.
- **Glasses.** For reading only; not usually worn.
- **Face.** Very expressive. Everything shows.
- **Language.** His English is the *Waves* English: idiomatic, heavily contracted, no
  carried-over syntax. **Keep it.** He is not a man speaking a second language
  awkwardly. What he does instead is speak Spanish sometimes — a word, a phrase, a
  whole sentence — and then carry on. It surfaces; it does not colour the baseline.
  Do not let a Spanish register creep into the English and formalise it: he is the
  least formal speaker on the station and must stay so.

## 8. The short version

> He tries to be warm and mostly manages it, and when somebody has put an animal or a
> person in danger the temper gets out. He is delighted by a good result and says so,
> and gives the credit away in front of witnesses. He contracts more than anyone on the
> station and generally does not compose sentences in advance. He does not back down when he is
> right, and does when he isn't.

## 9. Drafting controls (added revision e)

These are not characterisation. They are instruments for catching drift while it is
still cheap. All four are checkable before a file is handed over, and the numbers are
to be reported with the word count. §9.1 and §9.2 are the same on both sheets; §9.3 and
§9.4 are his.

### 9.1 The differential rule

**Pascual's contraction rate must exceed Reggie's in every installment.** Not by a
fixed margin — just always. This is the single most important line on either sheet.

§6 already documents one collapse and diagnoses it as a vacancy: a register left the
room with its owner and Pascual picked it up. The larger event was different and
slower. Over a long sequence the two men converged — Pascual's rate fell, Reggie's fell
further and then recovered, and by the end the difference between them was subject
matter and not voice. A rate floor does not catch that. A differential does.

This matters more for him than for anybody. His place in *Waves* is not "informal" in
the abstract; it is **the least formal speaker on the station**, measured against the
others. That is a relational fact and it needs a relational rule.

### 9.2 Per-installment budget

Measured before delivery. Reported with the word count. A file that fails is not handed
over; it is fixed first.

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| Pascual's contraction rate | ≥ 5.0 per 100 words of dialogue, **and above Reggie's** |
| Reggie's rate | ≥ 4.0 |
| Turns of 50+ words | max 2 per installment |
| Volleys (4+ consecutive short exchanges) | min 3 per installment |
| "eleven" as a quantity | max 2, and only where the clinical record is meant |
| Prolepsis (narration reporting the future) | max 1 |

The 50-word cap does most of its work on him. §3 says he does not deliver prepared
statements — he recounts, grumbles, interrupts himself, and asks four questions at once
when alarmed. *Waves* gives him no turn over sixty words at all. Every long turn he has
been given since is a scene in which he composed something in advance, and the cap is
the cheapest available guard against it.

### 9.3 Warmth is the default, not management

**The failure mode is not coldness. It is strategy.**

§1 sets the disposition: he tries to be warm, mostly manages it, and the temper is what
happens when the default fails. What replaced it over a long sequence was neither — it
was a man observing his own procedure and reporting the mechanism afterwards. Deciding
in advance how long to stay furious. Withholding a question on a calculated schedule.
Watching himself reach for the largest object in the room and giving an account of it
later.

Any one of those is a good scene. Together they make him a strategist, which is the
other man's faculty, and the two of them end up holding one set of tools.

The corrective is not to make him simpler. It is that **his insight arrives as verdict
or as action, and rarely as account.** He knows things fast and says them short. When he does need to explain himself, it is not polished — because a polished explanation is a performance, and he knows
it, and would rather not.

### 9.4 The absolutist construction

**Neither man says "I always" or "I never" about himself.**

In *Waves* the construction appears three times in nineteen thousand words and is never
once a totalising self-description. In the sequels the strong form — *I have never
once* / *I have never in my life* — runs to twenty-four instances, split between the
two of them, which is itself part of how they came to sound alike.

A man who says *I have never once* has stopped recounting and started summarising, and
a summary of a life is a diagnosis. For Pascual it is doubly wrong: §3 says he does not
compose in advance, and the construction is composition — nobody arrives at *I have
never in my life* without having drafted it.

Not a softened version either. *Almost never*, *I don't think I've ever*, *in five years
I have not once* — same move, hedged. The replacement is the specific instance: what he
did, when, in the room it happened in. Let the reader do the totalling.

The construction is not made acceptable by the text knowing it might be self-delusion.
Signalling the unreliability keeps the sentence and adds a wink. Prefer not to write it.
